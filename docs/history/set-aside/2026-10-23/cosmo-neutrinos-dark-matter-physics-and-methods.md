## Line 202 (Section 3.2, Half-mode scale and the keV conversion)

What this explains [der: the relations above evaluated at the published limits]: 6.5 keV in Eq. 18 of arXiv:2511.07513 gives 10^7.41 M⊙ and 5.6 keV in Eq. 10 of arXiv:2511.07765 gives 10^7.80 M⊙, so each JWST paper is internally consistent; but 10^7.8 M⊙ inserted in the arcs paper's relation would give about 5.0 keV rather than 5.6 keV. Part of the keV spread between the two JWST papers is the conversion, not the data. The two satellite relations (4.3 and 4.0 × 10⁸ M⊙ at 3 keV, same slope) differ by 0.03 dex. The pairs of Nadler et al. 2021 (10^7.4 M⊙ ↔ 7.4 keV, 10^7.0 M⊙ ↔ 9.7 keV) come from the 5 × 10⁸ (m/3 keV)^(−10/3) M⊙ relation, which that paper takes from Nadler et al. 2020 (arXiv:2101.07810 Sect. 2.4 [src]); the same section gives the subhalo suppression as [1 + (α M_hm/M)^β]^γ with (α, β, γ) = (2.7, 1.0, −0.99) in the 2020 satellite analysis and (1, 1, −1.3) in the 2019 lensing analysis [src]. The 2020 satellite, 2021 joint and 2025 flux-ratio limits thus use the −10/3 relation, the 2025 arcs and Milky Way + M31 papers the −3.564 one; the relation behind the 2019 pair (10^7.8 M⊙ ↔ 5.2 keV) was not read. Liu, Gong & Liao caution that comparing different non-cold models through M_hm "can be an oversimplification and may introduce significant biases" (arXiv:2512.01361 Sect. II.1 [src]).
