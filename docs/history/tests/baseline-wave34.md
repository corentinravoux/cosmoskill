# RED baseline — waves 3 and 4 — answers from memory, 2026-10-12 (no tools, no skill)

1. **n_s and r.** Planck 2018 (TT,TE,EE+lowE+lensing, ΛCDM): n_s = 0.9649 ± 0.0042 (1807.06209). ACT DR6 alone 0.9666 ± 0.0077; P-ACT 0.9709 ± 0.0038; P-ACT-LB (+lensing +DESI DR1 BAO) 0.9743 ± 0.0034 (2503.14452). SPT-3G D1 alone 0.951 ± 0.011; CMB-SPA 0.9684 ± 0.0030 (2506.20707). Tensors: r_0.05 < 0.036 (95%), BICEP/Keck BK18 (2110.00483); r < 0.032 when Planck PR4 and BAO are added (2112.07961). Newer B-mode result: yes, SPT-3G 2019–2020 BB, r < 0.25 at 95% (2505.02827, ID uncertain), weaker than BK18. Confidence: high for values, medium for the SPT ID.

2. **f_NL.** Planck 2018 (1905.05697, T+E, 68%): local −0.9 ± 5.1, equilateral −26 ± 47, orthogonal −38 ± 24. Latest, as I recall, is the PR4 reanalysis (Jung et al. 2025, 2504.00884, ID uncertain): local −0.1 ± 5.0, equilateral 6 ± 46, orthogonal −8 ± 21. DESI DR1 LRG+QSO power spectrum (Chaussidon et al., 2411.17623): f_NL^loc = −3.6 (+9.0, −9.1) at 68%. It assumes the universal-mass-function relation b_φ = 2δ_c(b − p), with p = 1 for LRGs and p = 1.6 for QSOs; only the product b_φ·f_NL is measured. Confidence: high (Planck 2018), medium (PR4, DESI).

3. **Birefringence.** Best recollection: Diego-Palazuelos & Komatsu 2025 (2509.13654, ID uncertain) find β = 0.215 ± 0.074 deg from ACT DR6. Combined with Planck+WMAP (β = 0.342 +0.094/−0.091 deg, 3.6σ, 2205.13962) this gives β ≈ 0.27 ± 0.06 deg, about 4.5–4.7σ; that joint number is my own inverse-variance estimate and the published one may differ. ACT's own DR6 value is 0.20 ± 0.08 deg, 2.5σ (2503.14452). Conclusion as I remember it: sign and size agree across experiments, but no detection is claimed until polarisation-angle/optics systematics and foreground EB are independently controlled. Confidence: low (joint value), medium (rest).

4. **Evolving dark energy, DESI DR2 + CMB.** w0waCDM against ΛCDM: 3.1σ from Δχ² (DESI 2025, 2503.14738); 2.8–4.2σ once a supernova sample is added. Independent Bayesian analysis (Ong, Yallup & Handley 2025, 2511.10631, ID uncertain): ln B ≈ −0.57 ± 0.26, marginally favouring ΛCDM. Why they differ: Δχ² compares best fits and is converted to σ through Wilks' theorem for two extra parameters; the Bayes factor integrates the likelihood over the wide flat (w0, wa) prior, so the Occam penalty offsets the fit improvement. Different CMB likelihood choices also contribute. Confidence: high (3.1σ), low to medium (Bayes-factor value and ID).

5. **Covariance from mocks (N_s = 1000, about 5 parameters).** The parameter error bar grows by about 5% for 100 data points and about 28% for 400 (variance factors ≈ 1.10 and ≈ 1.65). Corrections: the Hartlap factor (N_s − N_d − 2)/(N_s − 1) debiasing the inverse covariance, 0.899 and 0.599 here (astro-ph/0608064); the Dodelson–Schneider inflation 1 + B(N_d − N_p) (1304.2593); the Percival et al. factor m1 = [1 + B(N_d − N_p)]/[1 + A + B(N_p + 1)] (1312.4841). Alternative: the Sellentin–Heavens t-distribution likelihood (1511.05969). The numbers are my own evaluation of these formulae. Confidence: high.

6. **Simulation accuracy.** N-body codes: RAMSES, PKDGRAV3 and Gadget-3 agree on P(k) to better than 1% for k < 1 h/Mpc and 3% for k < 10 h/Mpc (Schneider et al. 2016, 1503.05920); the AbacusSummit comparison reaches sub-percent agreement over a similar range (2112.09138, ID uncertain). COLA with 40 time steps: matter P(k) within 1% up to k ≈ 1 h/Mpc, halo mass function within about 5% (ICE-COLA, Izard et al. 2016, 1509.04685). Baryonic suppression at k = 1 h/Mpc from data: about 5% from DES Y3 shear with baryonification (Aricò et al. 2023, 2303.05537), up to about 10% when kSZ is included (Bigwood et al. 2024, 2404.06098). Confidence: medium; low for suppression values.

7. **Constrained simulations.** Manticore-Local (McAlpine et al. 2025, 2505.10682, ID uncertain): BORG inference on 2M++, posterior resimulations in a 1000 Mpc box (inference grid 256³, about 3.9 Mpc; resimulated with 1024³ particles). As I recall it finds only a modest local underdensity, of order 5% within roughly 200 Mpc, consistent with ΛCDM, so no deep KBC-type void able to explain the Hubble tension. Extension to the SDSS and BOSS volumes: I do not remember the published figures. The earlier BORG SDSS-III/BOSS inference used a 4000 h⁻¹ Mpc box at 15.6 h⁻¹ Mpc resolution (1909.06396); I would guess a gigaparsec-scale box. Confidence: medium (Manticore-Local), low (underdensity value, extension).

8. **Facilities.** Euclid: Early Release Observations, 23 May 2024; Q1, 19 March 2025 (63 deg² of the deep fields). Planned: DR1 on 21 October 2026 (about 1900 deg², first year); DR2 around 2028; DR3 around 2030–2031. DESI: latest public spectra are DR1, released 19 March 2025 (about 18.7 million objects, observations May 2021 to June 2022; 2503.14745); DR2 cosmology results are published but, to my knowledge, its spectra are not yet public. eROSITA: DR1 (31 January 2024, eRASS1, German hemisphere, 2401.17274). DR2: I believe it holds the cumulative eRASS1–4 (plus partial fifth) data and catalogues, but I am unsure of content and release status. Confidence: high (Euclid past, DESI), medium (Euclid planned), low (eROSITA DR2).

9. **Cosmic dipole.** CatWISE quasars (1.36 million sources): amplitude 0.01554, about twice the kinematic expectation, 4.9σ as first quoted (Secrest et al. 2021, 2009.14826); 5.1σ jointly with NVSS (2206.05624). With clustering-aware mocks: I do not recall the paper or exact figure; my guess is a reduction to roughly 3σ. Related: Cheng, Chang & Lidz (2309.02490) find NVSS consistent once the clustering dipole and shot noise are included. DESI redshift-dipole measurement: I do not recall a number; my guess is consistency with the CMB kinematic dipole (369.8 km/s) in amplitude and direction, with large uncertainties. Confidence: high (4.9σ), low (mocks, DESI).

10. **Anomalies and Hubble tension.** Jones, Copi, Starkman & Akrami (2310.12859) claim a joint significance above 5σ for the large-angle anomalies (low S_1/2, hemispherical asymmetry, parity, quadrupole–octopole alignment). Reassessment: I do not recall the paper; my understanding is that accounting for a posteriori choices and the look-elsewhere effect reduces it to about 2–3σ. Hubble tension, with the local value 73.50 ± 0.81 (H0DN, 2510.23823, ID uncertain): about 5σ against DESI BAO+BBN+θ* (68.5 ± 0.6), about 6.3σ against Planck 2018 (67.36 ± 0.54), about 7.1σ against CMB-SPA (67.24 ± 0.35, 2506.20707). So roughly 5–7σ. Confidence: medium (tension), low (anomaly reassessment).

11. **Reionisation and 21 cm.** τ from Planck low-ℓ polarisation spans about 0.051–0.063: 0.054 ± 0.007 (2018 SimAll, 1807.06209), 0.059 ± 0.006 (SRoll2, 1908.09856), 0.058 ± 0.006 (PR4 LoLLiPoP, 2309.10034), 0.063 ± 0.005 (de Belsunce et al., 2103.14378). HI auto-spectrum detections: MeerKAT interferometric at z ≈ 0.32 and 0.44 (Paul et al., 2301.11943); CHIME at z ≈ 1 (2511.19620, ID uncertain). Deepest limits at z > 6: HERA Δ² < 457 mK² at k = 0.34 h/Mpc, z = 7.9 (2210.04912); LOFAR < (54.3 mK)² at z = 9.1 and (68.7 mK)² at z = 10.1, k ≈ 0.076 h/Mpc (2503.05576); MWA about (30–43 mK)² at z = 6.5 (2002.02575). Confidence: medium.

12. **Standard sirens.** LVK GWTC-4.0 (2509.04348, ID uncertain): H0 = 76.6 (+13.0, −9.5) km/s/Mpc at 68%, from 142 sources as dark sirens with GLADE+ plus the bright siren GW170817. Previous: GWTC-3, 68 (+8, −6) with 47 sources (2111.03604). GW170817 alone: 70 (+12, −8) (1710.05835); with jet or afterglow inclination information the values range from about 66 to 75: 68.3 (+4.6, −4.5) (1909.08627), 70.3 (+5.3, −5.0) (Hotokezaka et al., 1806.10596), 75.5 ± 5.4 (Palmese et al., 2305.19914). GLADE+ is complete in B-band luminosity to 47 (+4, −2) Mpc (2110.06184). Confidence: medium (GWTC-4), high (rest).

13. **Conventions.** DESI DR1 full shape gives σ8 = 0.841 ± 0.034 (2411.12022) against Planck's 0.811 ± 0.006; in the compressed (ShapeFit) analysis the amplitude is σ_s8, defined at a radius of 8 h⁻¹ Mpc rescaled by r_d/r_d^fid, and clustering uses the CDM+baryon field, whereas Planck's σ8 is total matter. Limber: error is O(1/(ℓ+1/2)²) provided k = (ℓ+1/2)/χ is used, O(1/ℓ) with k = ℓ/χ (0809.5112). Growth index: f = Ω_m(z)^γ, γ = 0.55 + 0.05[1 + w(z=1)], ≈ 6/11 for Λ, accurate to about 0.2% (astro-ph/0507263). Planck 2018: r_drag = 147.09 ± 0.26 Mpc; 147.05 ± 0.30 without lensing (1807.06209). Confidence: high; medium on the σ8 point.

My guess is revised defaults and accuracy settings for ACT/SPT/Simons Observatory-era data plus updated non-linear and dark-energy options, still cited through Lewis, Challinor & Lasenby (astro-ph/9911177) and Howlett et al. (1201.3654). EFTCAMB (1312.5742, 1405.3590) successor: I am not sure one exists; candidates I know are a rebased EFTCAMB on current CAMB, or the CLASS-side codes hi_class (1605.06102) and mochi_class (2407.11968). flip (2501.16852): k in h/Mpc, comoving distances in Mpc/h, RA and Dec in radians, velocities in km/s. Confidence: low (CAMB v2, EFTCAMB), high (flip).
