## 13. Derivation of the correction factors for a mock covariance; number of mocks for a 300-point data vector

**Answer:** There is no `deep/` dossier in this skill, so no worked derivation is available; what follows is the L2 chain of results, each with the paper that derives it. Notation: n_s mocks, n_d data points, n_θ parameters, S the sample covariance (Wishart, n_s − 1 degrees of freedom).

1. **Hartlap 2007** (arXiv:astro-ph/0608064): S⁻¹ is a biased estimate of Σ⁻¹. Unbiased precision Ψ = S⁻¹/h, h = (n_s − 1)/(n_s − n_d − 2). This fixes the bias of the precision only.
2. **Dodelson–Schneider 2013** (arXiv:1304.2593): the noise in Ψ scatters the best fit. V = [1 + B(n_d − n_θ)] F⁻¹, with B = (n_s − n_d − 2)/[(n_s − n_d − 1)(n_s − n_d − 4)].
3. **Percival 2014** (arXiv:1312.4841): the width read from the likelihood is biased by a different amount; rescale the parameter covariance by m_1 = [1 + B(n_d − n_θ)]/[1 + A + B(n_θ + 1)]. The constant A is not transcribed in the skill.
4. **Sellentin–Heavens 2016** (arXiv:1511.05969): marginalising over the unknown Σ with an independence-Jeffreys prior gives a multivariate t-distribution, ∝ [1 + χ²/(n_s − 1)]^(−n_s/2). It does not contain the Dodelson–Schneider term.
5. **Percival 2022** (arXiv:2108.10402): a prior chosen so that credible intervals have frequentist coverage gives ∝ [1 + χ²/(n_s − 1)]^(−m/2), m = n_θ + 2 + [n_s − 1 + B(n_d − n_θ)]/[1 + B(n_d − n_θ)]; Gaussian approximation: use S′ = (n_s − 1)[1 + B(n_d − n_θ)]/(n_s − n_d + n_θ − 1) × S, with no Hartlap factor on S.

Choosing n_s for n_d = 300: the requirement of Taylor, Joachimi & Kitching 2013 (arXiv:1212.4359) for N_D ≫ 100 is N_S > N_D and a covariance accurate to better than sqrt(2/N_D) = 8% [der]. In practice choose n_s from the error-bar inflation you accept, sqrt(S′/S), here for n_θ = 10 (my assumption; the question gives none) [der, computed here from the formula of step 5]:

| n_s | 600 | 1000 | 1500 | 2000 | 3000 | 5000 | 10000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| error-bar factor | 1.96 | 1.41 | 1.24 | 1.17 | 1.11 | 1.06 | 1.03 |

So about 3000 mocks for a 10% penalty and 5000–6000 for 5%; below ~1000 the correction dominates. Compressing the data vector lowers the requirement, since the number of mocks needed scales with the compressed dimension (arXiv:2108.10402, appendix).
`[cosmoskill L2 · base · cosmo-statistics-inference/references/physics-and-methods.md#sample-covariance-from-mocks-and-its-corrections]`
