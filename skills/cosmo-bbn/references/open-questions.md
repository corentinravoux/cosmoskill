## 1. The deuterium–CMB baryon-density discrepancy
<!-- key: the-deuterium-cmb-baryon-density-discrepancy -->

**Claim.** The measured D/H, (2.527 ± 0.030) × 10⁻⁵ (arXiv:1710.11129),
lies above the standard-BBN prediction at the CMB baryon density in several
current predictions: ≃1.8σ with PRIMAT (Pitrou et al. 2021,
arXiv:2011.11320) [src]; 1.70σ with Gaussian-process rates at the Planck
baryon density and 1.98σ with Planck + ACT DR6 + SPT-3G D1 (Launders,
Giovanetti & Liu 2026, arXiv:2604.16600) [src]; ~2.2σ against the
mean of all 17 literature values, ~1.4σ for the nine systems meeting the Cooke et al. 2014 criteria (Kislitsyn et al. 2024, arXiv:2401.12797) [src: Sect. 4.1]; "~2σ"
per Poulin et al. 2026 (arXiv:2607.20635) [src].

**Other side.** With data-driven rate fits the same data are concordant:
Yeh, Olive & Fields (2021) predict (2.51 ± 0.11) × 10⁻⁵ and find
"consistency among BBN theory, deuterium and ⁴He observations, and the
CMB" (arXiv:2011.13874) [src]; Pisanti et al. (2021) find D/H after LUNA
pointing to Ω_b h² "in excellent agreement" with Planck (arXiv:2011.11537)
[src]; Yeh et al. (2026) find BBN and CMB "consistent with each other"
(arXiv:2601.22239) [src]. Marginalising over rates widens the light-element
Ω_b h² to ±0.00055 and covers both rate philosophies (arXiv:2401.15054)
[src: Sect. 4.1].

**What decides it.** The tension is a statement about the deuterium-burning
rates more than about the abundances: new d(d,n)³He and d(d,p)t S-factors
at 0.1–0.6 MeV (arXiv:2604.16600) [src: abstract], and a resolution of the
d(p,γ)³He experiment–theory offset (§2). Launders et al. find that
low-degree polynomial fits, the technique of the data-driven networks,
systematically over-predict D/H in validation (arXiv:2604.16600) [src].

## 2. The d(p,γ)³He rate: experiment versus ab initio theory
<!-- key: the-d-p-gamma-3he-rate-experiment-versus-ab -->

LUNA measured d(p,γ)³He at BBN energies with a setup systematic below 3%
(arXiv:2005.00002; doi:10.1038/s41586-020-2878-4) [src]. Its data sit below
the ab-initio benchmark, and "BBN reaction networks split on which to
adopt" (Tait 2026, arXiv:2607.05514) [src]. An EFT fit gives
S(0) = 0.209 ± 0.008 eV b and attributes the offset to one natural-sized
contact term, equivalently a ~15% lower effective ³He asymptotic
normalisation (arXiv:2607.05514) [src]. Before LUNA, the choice between
the Marcucci ab-initio and Adelberger empirical rates moved 100 Ω_b h²
from 2.166 to 2.235 (arXiv:1710.11129) [src: Eqs. 12, 14]; after LUNA, the
PArthENoPE Ω_b h² moved by −0.9σ (arXiv:2401.15054) [src: Sect. 4.1].

## 3. The neutron-lifetime anomaly
<!-- key: the-neutron-lifetime-anomaly -->

Beam measurements average 888.0(2.0) s and trapped-UCN measurements
879.4(6) s, ~4σ apart (arXiv:1802.01804) [src]. The most precise bottle,
UCNτ, gives 877.75 ± 0.28 (+0.22/−0.16) s (arXiv:2106.10375) [src]; the
NIST beam 887.7 ± 1.2 ± 1.9 s (arXiv:1309.2623) [src]. The J-PARC beam
experiment, counting electrons rather than protons, gives
877.2 ± 1.7 (+4.0/−3.6) s, consistent with bottles and 2.3σ from the
proton-counting beam average (arXiv:2412.19519) [src] — which points to a
systematic in proton-counting beams rather than to new physics, but the
J-PARC error is still larger than the gap. Dark-decay explanations are
excluded with high confidence by decay-correlation data
(arXiv:1812.00626) [src]; the PNPI group argues for a beam-side
explanation (Serebrov et al. 2020, arXiv:2011.13272) [src: abstract
snippet]. For BBN the spread matters only for Y_P and remains below the
observational Y_P error; BBN + CMB "predict" 870 ± 16 s
(arXiv:2303.04140) [src].

## 4. The lithium problem
<!-- key: the-lithium-problem -->

The prediction exceeds the plateau: Pitrou et al. describe a "factor ∼3
discrepancy" (arXiv:2011.11320) [src: Sect. 1]. Three classes of solution
remain under discussion (Fields 2012, arXiv:1203.3551) [src: abstract]:

- **Stellar depletion.** Diffusion with extra mixing accounts for
  ~0.3 dex, leaving ~0.15 dex (arXiv:2111.00913) [src]; destruction during
  early star formation produces the "meltdown" below [Fe/H] ≈ −3
  (arXiv:2303.11497) [src]; warm GALAH dwarfs show an elevated plateau
  "largely consistent" with BBN (arXiv:2006.05173) [src]. Many but not all
  of the community suspect stellar destruction (Cooke 2024,
  arXiv:2409.06015) [src: Sect. 4.2].
- **Nuclear physics.** The ⁷Be neutron-destruction channels measured at
  n_TOF play a minor role (arXiv:1606.09420, arXiv:1803.05701) [src].
- **New physics.** Any solution must keep D/H, Y_P and ³He/⁴He at their
  Standard-Model values, which is "increasingly difficult"
  (arXiv:2203.11256) [src]; a two-step decay scenario shows it is possible
  in principle (Ganguly et al. 2026, arXiv:2604.01324) [src].

Independent tests: interstellar ⁷Li in the SMC near the BBN value
(arXiv:1207.3081) [src]; the plateau in ω Cen (arXiv:1008.1817) [src].

## 5. Helium systematics and N_eff from BBN
<!-- key: helium-systematics-and-neff-from-bbn -->

Measured Y_P spans 0.2370 (EMPRESS VIII) to 0.2551 (Izotov et al. 2014)
with quoted errors of 0.0013–0.0040 (`results.md` §2). EMPRESS implies
N_eff = 2.54 (+0.20/−0.25), ~2σ below the Standard Model, or a lepton
asymmetry ξ_e ≈ 0.05 (arXiv:2506.24050) [src]; Escudero et al. find
ξ_νe 2.2–2.9σ from zero on that basis (arXiv:2208.03201) [src]. Schöneberg
(2024) finds the EMPRESS value incompatible with D/H at ΔN_eff = 0
(χ²_min ≃ 4.6 against ~0 with PDG values) and treats it as an outlier
"until further evidence emerges" (arXiv:2401.15054) [src: Sect. 4.3]. The
LBT Y_p Project's 0.2458 ± 0.0013 from a weighted average of 15
low-metallicity targets (arXiv:2601.22238) [src] agrees with standard BBN;
whether a weighted average or a regression is appropriate, and how much
intrinsic scatter there is, are the method questions (§ systematics 2).
Burns (2026) shows that once ΔN_eff is free it dominates the theory
error on Y_P (arXiv:2603.22414) [src].

## 6. BBN and the Hubble tension
<!-- key: bbn-and-the-hubble-tension -->

- Early-time solutions to the Hubble tension generically raise the
  preferred ω_b, putting them in tension with deuterium; adding a D/H
  likelihood stops two representative models from reaching high H0
  (Giovanetti 2026, arXiv:2604.05095) [src].
- The D/H discrepancy grows to ~3σ in EDE-type models; a short increase of
  the expansion rate during deuterium burning (ΔH/H = 0.087 +0.036/−0.037)
  removes it, extra radiation does not (Poulin et al. 2026,
  arXiv:2607.20635) [src: abstract].
- The significance of the Hubble tension in an EDE model changes by 0.8σ
  with the treatment of rates and abundance data (Takahashi & Toda 2023,
  arXiv:2306.00454) [src: abstract].
- BAO + BBN H0 values and the BBN prior on r_d are held in
  `cosmo-distance-ladder-h0` and `cosmo-galaxy-clustering`; the H0 tension
  overview in `cosmo-tensions-anomalies`.
