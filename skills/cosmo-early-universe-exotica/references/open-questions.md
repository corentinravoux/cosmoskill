Snapshot: 2026-10-16

## 1. Astrophysical or cosmological origin of the PTA signal
<!-- key: astrophysical-or-cosmological-origin-of-the-pta -->

**The astrophysical side.** Astrophysically motivated SMBHB population models
reproduce both the amplitude and the shape of the NANOGrav spectrum, but the
amplitude "necessitates either a large number of parameters to be at the
edges of expected values, or a small number of parameters to be notably
different from standard expectations" (NANOGrav, arXiv:2306.16220) [src].
EPTA finds the signal consistent with SMBHBs and uses its relatively high
amplitude to constrain merger time-scales and black-hole–host scaling
relations (arXiv:2306.16227) [src].

**The amplitude problem.** Sato-Polito et al.
(arXiv:2312.06756) find that the background predicted from the present-day
SMBH abundance (local scaling relations) is "significantly smaller" than
measured; changes of merger history cannot close the gap, and preserving the
Soltan-argument mass density requires typical contributing masses of at
least ~3 × 10¹⁰ M☉, about ten times larger than predicted [src].

**The cosmological side.** In NANOGrav's new-physics search, inflation,
scalar-induced GWs, first-order phase transitions, metastable or
superstring cosmic strings and domain walls all reproduce the signal; only
stable field-theory cosmic strings fail. Many cosmological models give Bayes
factors of 10 to 100 against the SMBHB interpretation, but the collaboration
states that these "strongly depend on modeling assumptions about the cosmic
SMBHB population and, at this stage, should not be regarded as evidence for
new physics" (arXiv:2306.16219) [src]. EPTA reaches the same conclusion: "it
is impossible to discriminate among the different origins" at this stage;
early-Universe sources other than strings and phase transitions "would
require non-standard scenarios", such as a blue-tilted tensor spectrum or
enhanced small-scale scalar power (arXiv:2306.16227) [src].

**What decides it.** The spectral shape (the fitted γ ≈ 3.2 sits below 13/3,
arXiv:2306.16213), anisotropy and individual resolvable binaries are the
discriminants named by the collaborations: "higher-order features of the GWB
spectrum will definitively determine the nature of the GWB"
(arXiv:2306.16220) [src]. Because γ is fragile to the noise model of a few
pulsars (arXiv:2306.16213) [src: Sect. I], a cosmological claim resting on
γ < 13/3 is premature. The excluded-parameter regions, where a cosmological
model would overshoot the measured signal, hold whatever the origin of the
signal (arXiv:2306.16219) [src].

## 2. Primordial black holes as dark matter and as LVK sources
<!-- key: primordial-black-holes-as-dark-matter-and-as -->

**PBH binaries and LVK.** After GW150914, two groups proposed PBH mergers as the source: Bird et
al. 2016 (arXiv:1603.00464), with PBHs as all the dark matter in a 20–100 M☉ window and binaries
formed in halo encounters [src]; Sasaki et al. 2016 (arXiv:1603.08338), with binaries formed in
the early Universe, where PBHs as all the dark matter would exceed the observed rate and a
fraction of the dark matter matches it [src]. The two formation channels predict different
rates.

- **Stellar-mass PBHs as the LVK population.** The 2016 proposals
  (arXiv:1603.00464, arXiv:1603.08338) [src] are now confronted with
  f_PBH ≲ 10⁻³ over 1–200 M☉ from O3 population analyses
  (arXiv:2405.05732) and < 1–10% from twenty years of OGLE LMC data
  (arXiv:2403.02386) [src]. PBHs can still contribute a sub-population of
  mergers without being the dark matter.
- **The OGLE versus MACHO dispute.** OGLE finds no long events and fewer
  events than expected from known stars; Hawkins (arXiv:2509.05400) argues
  that passbands, light-curve quality, selection, efficiency, self-lensing
  and halo models explain the discrepancy with MACHO's halo lenses and that
  OGLE constrains nothing [src]. The Carr et al. review flags that "the
  validity of the recent analysis by the OGLE collaboration has been
  challenged" (arXiv:2601.06024) [src: Sect. IV.2.1]. Both sides are
  cited here; neither has conceded.
- **Ultrashort bulge events.** The Earth-mass PBH reading (arXiv:1901.07120)
  [src] is ruled out by the OGLE Magellanic Cloud high-cadence survey, which
  attributes the events to free-floating or wide-orbit planets
  (arXiv:2410.06251) [src].
- **The asteroid-mass window** is the open space for PBHs as all the dark
  matter (arXiv:2601.06024) [src: Sect. V].

## 3. Early dark energy and modified recombination
<!-- key: early-dark-energy-and-modified-recombination -->

The status of these models as H0 resolutions is the business of
`cosmo-tensions-anomalies`; what is open here is physics and method.

- **Is there an EDE signal at all?** ACT DR6 alone gives f_EDE < 0.088 and
  no H0 shift, erasing the DR4 hint (arXiv:2503.14454) [src: Sect. V.1];
  SPT-3G sees none from CMB alone (arXiv:2507.23355) [src]. The mild
  preferences appear only with DESI BAO, which SPT-3G reads as the
  CMB–DESI discrepancy in ΛCDM [src].
- **Bayesian limits or profile intervals?** Profile likelihoods give
  non-zero f_EDE (0.072 ± 0.036, arXiv:2112.12140; 0.09 ± 0.03,
  arXiv:2505.08051) [src] where Bayesian analyses give upper limits. Both
  are correct answers to different questions; a referee should ask which
  one a paper reports.
- **Large-scale structure.** The S8 penalty of EDE (arXiv:2003.07355) [src]
  is weighed in `cosmo-tensions-anomalies`.
- **Phenomenological constants.** The step-function m_e and α_EM models are
  not physical; first-principles implementations differ
  (arXiv:2503.14454) [src: Sect. V.2]. The 2.4σ low P-ACT m_e and the 2.5σ
  high P-ACT-LB α_EM are read by ACT as fluctuations [src: Sect. V.2]. The
  m_e + Ω_k route survives CMB + BAO but is pushed back by SNe Ia [src:
  Fig. 14 caption], and data-driven m_e(z) solutions fail once DESI DR2 is
  added (arXiv:2606.06495) [src].

## 4. Spectral distortions: what a next-generation instrument decides
<!-- key: spectral-distortions-what-a-next-generation -->

- **The ΛCDM μ.** Acoustic dissipation predicts μ ≃ 2.3 × 10⁻⁸; a
  detection extends the primordial spectrum by over three orders of
  magnitude in scale, and a non-detection would require new physics
  (arXiv:1909.01593) [src: Sect. 2.1]. PIXIE's foreground-limited σ(μ) ≃ 3
  × 10⁻⁸ does not reach it; Super-PIXIE (7.7 × 10⁻⁹) or FOSSIL would
  (arXiv:1909.01593 [src: Sect. 1.2]; arXiv:2609.16194 [src]) — all
  **forecasts**.
- **Distortions and the PTA signal.** For a log-normal curvature spectrum fitted to PTA data,
  FIRAS has insufficient sensitivity, but PIXIE-like experiments could detect the distortion of
  the SIGW interpretation (**forecast**; Tagliazucchi et al. 2023, arXiv:2310.08527) [src], so
  distortions can test that reading of the PTA signal independently of the SMBHB prior.
- **Post-recombination signatures.** Exotic injection leaves a residual
  distortion with a high-energy feature after subtraction of the standard
  background distortion, possibly detectable by PIXIE (**forecast**,
  arXiv:2303.07370) [src].

## 5. Primordial magnetic fields
<!-- key: primordial-magnetic-fields -->

- **A hint at recombination?** Jedamzik et al. 2025 find a 1.8–3σ preference
  for 5–10 pG fields with MHD-informed recombination (arXiv:2503.09599)
  [src]; ACT DR6 finds b < 0.41 in the three-zone model and "no evidence of
  clumping" (arXiv:2503.14454) [src: Fig. 15 caption]. ACT notes that a
  simulation-informed model agrees "remarkably" with the three-zone model
  where they can be compared [src: Sect. V.3]. The two statements use
  different data (Planck + DESI ± SNe versus ACT + Planck) and are not yet
  reconciled in one analysis.
- **A hint in the Lyα forest?** The B ~ 0.2 nG extra power of Pavičević et
  al. disappears under conservative noise modelling (arXiv:2501.06299)
  [src]. The forest data side is in `cosmo-lyman-alpha`.
- **Duty-cycle dependence of the γ-ray lower bound** (arXiv:2306.05132)
  [src] leaves the lower edge of the allowed window uncertain by two orders
  of magnitude.
