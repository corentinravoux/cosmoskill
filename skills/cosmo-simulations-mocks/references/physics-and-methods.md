# Physics and methods — cosmological simulations and mocks

Scope: the numerical methods behind simulations and mock catalogues used in survey cosmology, each with its reference paper.
Snapshot: 2026-10-13

Citation years in this skill are arXiv submission years, as in `sources.tsv`; the journal year is often one later.

## 1. N-body methods and codes
<!-- key: n-body-methods-and-codes -->

The reference review of collisionless simulations is Angulo & Hahn 2021 (arXiv:2112.05165). It covers the equations and their link to general relativity, the discretisation of phase space (N-body, Lagrangian submanifold, Schrödinger–Poisson), force calculation (direct summation, mesh, tree), initial conditions, extensions (baryons, neutrinos, primordial non-Gaussianity, modified gravity), and the path to observables (structure finding, emulators, light-cones). Read its section list before writing a methods paragraph.

A gravity-only simulation samples the cold-matter phase-space sheet with N particles of mass m_p = Ω_m ρ_crit L³ / N, softens the two-body force below a length ε, and integrates with a symplectic (kick–drift–kick) scheme. The codes differ in how they compute the force.

**Force calculation: the methods and the papers that introduced them.**

- **Particle mesh (PM).** Particle masses are assigned to a grid and the Poisson equation is solved with fast Fourier transforms, at a cost O(M log M) for M grid points; the method derives from the particle-in-cell technique of plasma physics and is among the oldest in cosmology (Doroshkevich et al. 1980 and the book of Hockney & Eastwood 1981, neither with an identifier here) [sec: arXiv:2112.05165, Sect. 5.1]. The mass-assignment kernels of order n = 1 to 4 (NGP, CIC, TSC, PCS) have the Fourier transform [sinc(π k / 2 k_Ny)]ⁿ per dimension, with k_Ny = π/Δx [src: Eq. 62 of arXiv:2112.05165]. Force resolution is the cell size, which is why pure PM is now the engine of fast mocks (section 5) and not of reference runs.
- **Tree.** Barnes & Hut 1986 (doi:10.1038/324446a0) partition space with a hierarchical tree and replace particle–particle by particle–node interactions expanded in multipoles, which brings the cost from O(N²) to O(N log N); a node of size r_ℓ at distance d is used unopened when r_ℓ/d < θ_c, the opening angle [sec: arXiv:2112.05165, Sect. 5.4 and Eq. 70]. The method returns the force at a requested accuracy, not to machine precision.
- **P³M and TreePM.** The potential is split with a kernel of scale r_s close to the grid spacing into a long-range part solved on the mesh and a short-range part summed in real space, by direct summation (P³M) or with a tree (TreePM) [sec: arXiv:2112.05165, Sect. 5.3, Eqs. 65–67]. Bagla 1999 (arXiv:astro-ph/9911025) introduced TreePM as the combination of the Barnes–Hut tree and a particle-mesh code, keeping the periodic boundary conditions of the mesh and the resolution of the tree [src]. GADGET (Springel, Yoshida & White 2000, arXiv:astro-ph/0003162) is a tree code with individual adaptive time steps and smoothed particle hydrodynamics; GADGET-2 (Springel 2005, arXiv:astro-ph/0505010) adds the optional TreePM split, an entropy-conserving SPH, a quasi-symplectic integrator and a space-filling-curve domain decomposition, and ran the first simulation with more than 10¹⁰ particles [src]. GreeM (Ishiyama et al. 2009, arXiv:0910.0121) is the massively parallel TreePM code [src] that ran Uchuu [sec: arXiv:2112.05165, Sect. 10.1].
- **Fast multipole method (FMM).** Proposed by Greengard & Rokhlin 1987 and brought to Cartesian coordinates by Dehnen 2002 (arXiv:astro-ph/0202512), whose code computes mutual cell–cell interactions by a symmetric Taylor expansion, conserves total momentum, scales as O(N) and is at least ten times faster than a standard tree code [src]. PKDGRAV, GADGET-4 and SWIFT adopted it [sec: arXiv:2112.05165, Sect. 5.4.2].
- **Adaptive mesh refinement (AMR).** RAMSES (Teyssier 2001, arXiv:astro-ph/0111367) refines a tree-structured grid cell by cell, with an N-body solver close to that of the ART code and a second-order Godunov hydrodynamic solver [src].
- **Near-field/far-field split of Abacus.** Particles closer than about 2L/K, for a grid of K³ cells, interact by direct summation and all others through a multipole representation of order p = 8; the review notes that the price is discontinuous force errors and the absence of Ewald corrections [sec: arXiv:2112.05165, Sect. 5.3].
- **HACC** (Habib et al. 2014, arXiv:1410.2805) is a framework built to run on all supercomputer architectures, with benchmarks above 3.6 trillion particles [src].

The GADGET-4 paper states that the code offers both a classic entropy-conserving SPH and a pressure-based SPH (arXiv:2010.03567). The 2026 code releases found by the date-sorted search are GPU-native: GINKAKU (above), gevolution 2.0 and the differentiable FMM code JZ-FMM appeared between May and September 2026; only GINKAKU was read in full and is cited here.

**Variance suppression.** "Fixed and paired" simulations fix the initial mode amplitudes to the ensemble mean and run a second box with opposite phases; a fixed pair can be as precise on non-linear scales as an average over 50 traditional simulations, for P(k), the redshift-space multipoles, the halo mass function and the reduced bispectrum at z = 1 [src] (Angulo & Pontzen 2016, arXiv:1603.05253). The UNIT project found the two- and three-point statistics unbiased, the variance of two-point statistics greatly reduced, and the variance of three-point statistics not significantly reduced [src] (Chuang et al. 2018, arXiv:1811.02111). A fixed-amplitude box therefore cannot be used to estimate a covariance. Zel'dovich control variates are the other variance-reduction technique in production: Aemulus ν reports effective-volume gains of 10 to 10⁵ depending on the statistic [src] (DeRose et al. 2023, arXiv:2303.09762).

## 2. Initial conditions
<!-- key: initial-conditions -->

Initial conditions displace particles from a grid or glass with Lagrangian perturbation theory (LPT) at a starting redshift z_i.

**Definitions, as the review writes them.** The displacement field of the Lagrangian map x = q + Ψ is expanded in the linear growth factor, Ψ(q, τ) = Σ_{j=1..n} D₊(τ)ʲ Ψ⁽ʲ⁾(q) [src: Eq. 75 of arXiv:2112.05165]. The first order is the Zel'dovich approximation, Ψ⁽¹⁾ = −∇φ⁽¹⁾ [src: Eq. 76] (Zel'dovich 1970, no identifier here). The second order is Ψ⁽²⁾ = ∇φ⁽²⁾ with φ⁽²⁾ = −(3/14) ∇⁻²[φ⁽¹⁾,ᵢᵢ φ⁽¹⁾,ⱼⱼ − φ⁽¹⁾,ᵢⱼ φ⁽¹⁾,ᵢⱼ] [src: Eq. 77]; at third order the displacement acquires a transverse part [src: Eq. 78]. First order captures the variance of the density distribution, second order the skewness, third order the kurtosis [sec: arXiv:2112.05165, Sect. 6.2].

**Foundations.**
- Bouchet et al. 1994 (arXiv:astro-ph/9406013): the systematic account of the Lagrangian perturbative expansion up to third order, for arbitrary Ω and for flat models with Λ; its linear term is the Zel'dovich solution, and second order is found "both simple and powerful" against N-body runs [src].
- Scoccimarro 1997 (arXiv:astro-ph/9711187): Zel'dovich initial conditions excite decaying modes because the approximation does not conserve momentum; the skewness S₃ reaches 10% accuracy only after an expansion by a factor 6 for spectral index n = 0, an Ω = 0.3 model needs twice the expansion of Ω = 1, and 2LPT removes the transient in the skewness and cuts the required expansion by more than an order of magnitude for higher moments [src]. The paper proposes 2LPT initial conditions and gives the steps to implement them.
- Jenkins 2009 (arXiv:0910.0258): 2LPT for multi-mass (zoom) particle loads; halo properties depend much less on the starting redshift than with Zel'dovich starts, which delay the collapse of overdense regions [src]. Jenkins 2013 (arXiv:1306.5968): Panphasia, a public hierarchical (octree) white-noise field spanning more than 10¹⁵ in linear scale, so that the phases of a run and of its zooms are published as a location in one realisation [src].
- Particle loads. A regular grid is gravitationally stable and noise-free but globally anisotropic; a glass, made by evolving a Poisson distribution under a repulsive force until it freezes (White 1994, arXiv:astro-ph/9410043, lecture notes whose abstract does not describe it), is more isotropic, with a white-noise spectrum below the mean inter-particle separation falling as k⁴ on larger scales, hence more noise than a lattice [sec: arXiv:2112.05165, Sect. 6.4].
- Discreteness. Near the particle Nyquist wavenumber the particle system does not follow the continuum growing mode. Garrison et al. 2016 (arXiv:1605.02333) build initial conditions on the growing modes of the particle lattice and rescale the amplitudes: 1% accuracy on P(k) down to k_Nyquist, against k_Nyquist/4 without the rescaling and k_Nyquist/13 without 2LPT, relative to an oversampled reference [src]. The scheme was developed with the Abacus code.
- Two fluids. Hahn, Rampf & Uhlemann 2020 (arXiv:2008.09124) generalise nLPT to baryons plus dark matter (2LPT or 3LPT two-fluid initial conditions, with particle-mass variations that remove spurious deviations) in the public monofonIC code [src]; FLAMINGO uses it.
- Variance suppression. "Pairing" (two runs with inverted initial density) is from Pontzen et al. 2015 (arXiv:1511.04090) [src]; "fixing" the amplitudes from Angulo & Pontzen 2016 (section 1). Villaescusa-Navarro et al. 2018 (arXiv:1806.01871) tested both on 600 N-body and 506 magneto-hydrodynamic runs: no bias in any of 19 quantities, variance of power spectra reduced by factors up to 10⁶, and no change in the scatter of the density PDF or of the halo, void and stellar mass functions [src].

Three more papers define current practice.

- Crocce, Pueblas & Scoccimarro 2006 (arXiv:astro-ph/0606505): Zel'dovich (1LPT) initial conditions excite long-lived transients. Started at z_i = 49, they underestimate the non-linear power spectrum by about 2, 4 and 8% at z = 0, 1 and 3, and the halo mass function by 5% at 10¹⁵ M_⊙/h at z = 0 [src]. 2LPT became the standard after this paper.
- Hahn & Abel 2011 (arXiv:1103.6031), the MUSIC code: multi-scale ("zoom") initial conditions by real-space convolution of white noise with the transfer function and a multigrid Poisson solver; RMS relative errors of order 10⁻⁴ on displacements and velocities in the refined region [src]. This is the reference for zoom-in initial conditions, including those used to resimulate a region of a constrained realisation.
- Michaux et al. 2020 (arXiv:2008.09588): with 3LPT, truncation errors are small enough to start as late as z = 12 at the resolution studied; late starts suppress particle-discreteness errors, which behave as decaying modes amplified by early starts. Late-start 3LPT agrees with the continuum fluid limit within 1% for the power spectrum and bispectrum at z = 0 up to the particle Nyquist wavenumber, k about 3 h/Mpc in their runs [src]. The recommendation is to start as late as possible with 3LPT, the opposite of the older "start early" habit.

Baryons and dark matter have different transfer functions; MUSIC generalises to two-component initial conditions and recommends the local Lagrangian approximation for the initial baryon density on mesh codes (arXiv:1103.6031). Suites that include neutrinos, radiation or dark-energy perturbations at linear order work in the N-body gauge (EuclidEmulator2, arXiv:2010.11288; GINKAKU, arXiv:2605.28581), so that Newtonian particle positions can be read in a relativistic framework.

Two flagship runs show how far practice is spread. FLAMINGO uses multi-fluid 3LPT initial conditions (monofonIC, modified for massive neutrinos) at z = 31, chosen "as late as possible to limit discreteness errors", in the N-body gauge, with partially fixed amplitudes (modes with (kL)² < 1025 set to the mean variance) and phases taken from Panphasia so that zoom resimulations are possible [src] (arXiv:2306.04024, section 2.4). Euclid Flagship 2 uses 1LPT at z = 99 [src] (arXiv:2405.13495, section 2.1).

## 3. Hydrodynamics and sub-grid models
<!-- key: hydrodynamics-and-sub-grid-models -->

Reviews: Vogelsberger et al. 2019 (arXiv:1909.07976) for methodology; Crain & van de Voort 2023 (arXiv:2309.17075) for what the galaxy-formation suites get right and where they disagree; Chisari et al. 2019 (arXiv:1905.06082) for the consequences for survey cosmology.

**Hydrodynamic solvers in the suites cited here.** Moving mesh (AREPO: IllustrisTNG, MillenniumTNG, part of CAMELS; arXiv:1703.02970, arXiv:2210.10060, arXiv:2010.00619); smoothed particle hydrodynamics (implemented in GADGET-4 and SWIFT, arXiv:2010.03567, arXiv:2305.13380; FLAMINGO runs on SWIFT, arXiv:2306.04024 section 2.1); meshless finite mass (GIZMO: SIMBA and part of CAMELS; arXiv:1901.10203, arXiv:2010.00619); Eulerian finite-volume grid for the intergalactic medium (Nyx, section 4); adaptive mesh refinement (RAMSES, section 4). EAGLE and BAHAMAS run on versions of GADGET-3 (TreePM gravity, SPH), BAHAMAS with the hydrodynamics and sub-grid modules of OWLS and cosmo-OWLS unchanged [src: Sect. 3 of arXiv:1407.7040; Sect. 2.1 of arXiv:1603.02702].

**Foundations: the solvers.**
- Smoothed particle hydrodynamics in cosmological codes: GADGET and GADGET-2 (section 1), the latter with a formulation that conserves energy and entropy with adaptive smoothing lengths [src] (arXiv:astro-ph/0505010).
- Moving mesh: AREPO (Springel 2009, arXiv:0901.4107) solves the conservation laws with a finite-volume, second-order Godunov scheme on a Voronoi tessellation whose generating points move with the flow; the scheme is Galilean-invariant, adapts its resolution continuously like SPH and keeps the shock treatment of Eulerian codes [src].
- Mesh-free finite mass and finite volume: GIZMO (Hopkins 2014, arXiv:1409.7395) uses a kernel discretisation of the volume with a Riemann solver over the overlaps; it conserves mass, energy and momentum exactly, needs no artificial diffusion, and is found competitive with moving-mesh schemes at the cost of more noise [src].
- Adaptive mesh refinement: RAMSES (section 1), with a second-order Godunov solver [src] (arXiv:astro-ph/0111367).

**Foundations: the sub-grid recipes the suites descend from.**
- Star formation and stellar feedback: the hybrid multi-phase model of Springel & Hernquist 2002 (arXiv:astro-ph/0206393) treats unresolved star-forming gas as cold clouds in a hot medium, with thermal supernova heating, cloud evaporation and galactic winds; its quiescent mode has one free parameter, the star-formation time-scale, fixed on local disc galaxies, and the winds were added because the model otherwise overproduces stars [src].
- Black holes: Springel, Di Matteo & Hernquist 2004 (arXiv:astro-ph/0411108) accrete gas onto sink particles and couple a small fraction of the bolometric luminosity thermally to the gas, which regulates the growth [src]. Booth & Schaye 2009 (arXiv:0904.2572) modify it for cosmological volumes: Bondi–Hoyle rates must be boosted by large ad hoc factors because the multiphase medium is unresolved, which they identify as the largest source of uncertainty; black holes release a fixed energy for a given halo mass whatever the assumed efficiency [src].
- Thermal feedback without switching off cooling: Dalla Vecchia & Schaye 2012 (arXiv:1203.5667) trace the catastrophic cooling of injected supernova energy to heating too much mass, and inject it stochastically with a minimum temperature jump [src]. The EAGLE abstract describes its feedback in the same terms, thermal injection without turning cooling off (below).
- Suites that set the practice of varying the recipe: OWLS, more than fifty runs with one process changed at a time (Schaye et al. 2009, arXiv:0909.5196) [src]; cosmo-OWLS, its large-volume extension for groups and clusters, which found that some AGN models give a realistic group and cluster population while others are too violent although they reproduce the black-hole scaling relations, so that calibration is required (Le Brun et al. 2013, arXiv:1312.5462) [src]; Illustris, (106.5 Mpc)³ with 2 × 1820³ resolution elements, dark-matter particle mass 6.26 × 10⁶ M_⊙, initial baryonic mass 1.26 × 10⁶ M_⊙ and 710 pc softening at z = 0 (Vogelsberger et al. 2014, arXiv:1405.2921) [src].

**Sub-grid models.** Star formation, stellar evolution and enrichment, radiative cooling, stellar feedback, black-hole seeding, growth and feedback all act below the resolution and are implemented as parametrised recipes. Three statements from the reviews and suite papers set the level of trust:

1. The feedback efficiencies cannot be predicted from first principles and are calibrated. EAGLE calibrates to the z about 0 galaxy stellar mass function, the black-hole mass relation and galaxy sizes, and reproduces the mass function to within about 0.2 dex over 10⁸ < M_*/M_⊙ < 10¹¹ [src] (Schaye et al. 2014, arXiv:1407.7040).
2. Similar galaxy populations emerge from dissimilar feedback implementations, which predict markedly different gas flows into and out of haloes; circumgalactic and intragroup gas is where the suites differ [src] (arXiv:2309.17075).
3. For large-scale-structure cosmology the calibration target must include the gas content of groups and clusters, because that is what sets the matter power spectrum. BAHAMAS calibrates stellar and AGN feedback to the present-day stellar mass function and the hot gas mass fractions of groups and clusters (McCarthy et al. 2016, arXiv:1603.02702). FLAMINGO does the same with Gaussian-process emulators trained on Latin hypercubes of 32 small-volume runs, separately at three resolutions spanning a factor of 64 in particle mass, and defines its model variations by shifts of the calibration data rather than by sub-grid parameter values [src] (Schaye et al. 2023, arXiv:2306.04024; Kugel et al. 2023, arXiv:2306.05492).

**AGN feedback implementations named in the abstracts read.** EAGLE: thermal energy injection without switching off cooling (arXiv:1407.7040). IllustrisTNG: multi-mode black-hole feedback with a new kinetic mode at low accretion rates, revised galactic winds, and magnetohydrodynamics (Pillepich et al. 2017, arXiv:1703.02970). SIMBA: kinetic bipolar outflows of about 10³ km/s at high Eddington ratio rising to about 8000 km/s below 2%, plus X-ray energy; torque-limited accretion from cold gas (Davé et al. 2019, arXiv:1901.10203). FLAMINGO: thermal by default, collimated jets as an alternative (arXiv:2306.04024).

**How the baryonic effect on P(k) was established.**
- Jing et al. 2005 (arXiv:astro-ph/0512426): gravity-only against non-radiative and against cooling-plus-star-formation runs; the abstract mentions no AGN feedback. The total matter power is lower by up to 1% at 1 < k < 10 h/Mpc and higher at k about 20 h/Mpc, by 2% (non-radiative) and 10% (with star formation) [src].
- Rudd, Zentner & Kravtsov 2007 (arXiv:astro-ph/0703741): three runs from one initial field; the changes come from the rearrangement of matter inside haloes, not from a large-scale rearrangement, which motivates a halo-model correction [src].
- van Daalen et al. 2011 (arXiv:1104.1174, table below and `results.md` §4): with AGN feedback the sign and the size change, to a suppression of 10% at k about 1 h/Mpc.
- Semboloni et al. 2011 (arXiv:1105.1075): ignoring the effect of the OWLS AGN run biases w₀ by about 40% for a Euclid-like tomographic lensing survey; the modification is dominated by groups and clusters, and a halo model with gas and stellar fractions as a function of halo mass reduces the bias to the statistical error [src]. The review lists it first among the halo-model extensions for baryons [sec: arXiv:2112.05165, Sect. 9.9.5].

Which point of the model space nature occupies is the open question (see `open-questions.md` §1.1), and its consequences for S8 are treated in `cosmo-weak-lensing` and `cosmo-cmb-lss-cross-correlations`.

## 4. Hydrodynamical simulations for the Lyα forest
<!-- key: hydrodynamical-simulations-for-the-lyalpha -->

The forest at 2 < z < 5 comes from gas near the mean density, so the simulation problem differs from galaxy formation: uniform resolution of low-density gas matters more than sub-grid physics, and the thermal and ionisation history of the intergalactic medium replaces feedback as the main nuisance. The analyses themselves (P1D, P3D, BAO, thermal state, dark-matter limits) are in `cosmo-lyman-alpha`.

**Codes and the reason each is used.**

| Code | Method | Why it matters for the forest | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nyx | particle-mesh gravity; finite-volume Eulerian hydrodynamics on a grid | low-density gas, where the forest forms, is not under-sampled as it is by SPH particles (arXiv:2207.05023); 20 kpc/h cells give 1% convergence of flux statistics to k = 10 h/Mpc, and L > 40 Mpc/h suppresses missing-mode errors below 1% [src] | Lukić et al. 2014, arXiv:1406.6361 |
| CRK-HACC | Tree-PM gravity; conservative reproducing-kernel SPH | agrees with Nyx to ≲ 1% in P1D only when the density is reconstructed by tessellation instead of the SPH kernel [src] | Chabanier et al. 2022, arXiv:2207.05023 |
| SPH codes (code names not in the abstracts read; PRIYA uses "the code and hydrodynamic model of the ASTRID simulation") | particle-based hydrodynamics | Sherwood, Sherwood-Relics, PRIYA; allow galaxy-formation physics and warm dark matter in the same run | Bolton et al. 2016, arXiv:1605.03462; Bird et al. 2023, arXiv:2306.05471 |
| RAMSES | adaptive mesh refinement | Horizon-AGN series used to bound the AGN-feedback signature in P1D | Chabanier et al. 2020, arXiv:2002.02822 |

**Standard approximations and what each costs.**
- Optically thin, spatially uniform ultraviolet background (Haardt & Madau 2012 or Faucher-Giguère et al. 2009 tables). With the former, Nyx gives T_0 about 10⁴ K and γ about 1.55 at z = 2 and a mean flux close to observations; with the latter the mean flux is 10–15% low at z = 2 and a factor 2 low at z = 4 [src] (arXiv:1406.6361).
- Rescaling optical depths in post-processing to match an assumed mean flux. It is universal practice and it changes the apparent convergence rate [src] (arXiv:1406.6361). It is also why the amplitude of the linear power is degenerate with the mean transmission in P1D fits (Walther et al. 2024, arXiv:2412.05372).
- Splicing: combining a large low-resolution box with a small high-resolution box to emulate a large high-resolution one. Lukić et al. find it accurate only at the 10% level for P1D [src] (arXiv:1406.6361). Any analysis that still splices carries this as a model error.
- Instantaneous, homogeneous reionisation. Sherwood-Relics replaces it with a hybrid radiative-transfer scheme that follows the patchy ionising field; patchy reionisation leaves large-scale temperature fluctuations that persist after reionisation and raise the flux power on large scales at z > 4 [src] (Puchwein et al. 2022, arXiv:2207.13098). PRIYA includes patchy hydrogen and helium reionisation models and finds that patchy helium reionisation adds large-scale P1D power [src] (arXiv:2306.05471).
- No galaxy formation. AGN feedback suppresses the P1D at all scales, more at low redshift; ignoring it shifts σ_8 by 2% and n_s by 1% [src] (arXiv:2002.02822).

**Parametrising the three-dimensional flux power.** Arinyo-i-Prats et al. 2015 (arXiv:1506.04519) introduced the fitting formula for the non-linear P3D of the transmission, with a transition scale from linear to non-linear anisotropy and a pressure (Jeans) cut-off, and predicted the two linear biases b_δ and b_η from hydrodynamical simulations. The six small-scale parameters that ForestFlow emulates are deviations of this kind.

| ForestFlow (conditional normalising flow) | 30 fixed-and-paired hydrodynamical simulations, z = 2–4.5 | b_δ, b_η and six parameters of the small-scale P3D | 3% on P3D to k = 5 Mpc⁻¹ and 1.5% on P1D to k_∥ = 4 Mpc⁻¹ [src] | Chaves-Montero et al. 2024, arXiv:2409.05682 |
| PRIYA (multi-fidelity Gaussian process) | 48 low-fidelity + 3 high-fidelity runs | 4 cosmological + 5 astrophysical or thermal parameters | interpolation error < 1%; simulations converged at the percent level for z = 5.4–2.2 [src] | Bird et al. 2023, arXiv:2306.05471 |
| Lyssa / lym1d (Gaussian process) | 18 Nyx runs | A_Lyα, n_Lyα and thermal parameters | each run sub-percent converged in P1D; percent-level agreement with the previous emulator [src] | Walther et al. 2024, arXiv:2412.05372 |

Units differ between these emulators (Mpc⁻¹ for LaCE and ForestFlow, h/Mpc for Nyx convergence statements, s/km for data); convert before comparing a k_max.

**Synthetic spectra for BAO analyses.** Lyα BAO mocks are not hydrodynamical. LyaCoLoRe turns Gaussian-random-field skewers into transmitted-flux skewers with fast approximations, recovers the BAO signal and literature-like bias parameters, and can add high-column-density systems and metals (Farr et al. 2019, arXiv:1912.02763). The date-sorted search of 2025–2026 found hybrid-EFT and field-level forward models validated on AbacusSummit with a painted forest and on Sherwood (titles seen: arXiv IDs not cited because the abstracts were not read in full).

## 5. Approximate methods and fast mocks
<!-- key: approximate-methods-and-fast-mocks -->

Approximate methods exist because a covariance matrix or a field-level inference needs hundreds to millions of forward evaluations, and a tree code cannot supply them. They fall into four families, ordered by decreasing cost.

**5.1 Quasi-N-body particle-mesh schemes.** A particle-mesh solver with few global time steps, made accurate on large scales by building perturbation theory into the integrator.

| Scheme | Principle | Stated performance | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| COLA | solve the equations of motion in the frame comoving with the LPT trajectories; the N-body part only supplies the residual | 10 time steps suffice for halo statistics down to 10¹¹ M_⊙/h in a 100 Mpc/h box with 5 × 10⁹ M_⊙/h particles; the same accuracy without COLA needs at least 3 times more steps [src] | Tassev et al. 2013, arXiv:1301.0322 |
| L-PICOLA | parallel COLA code with a light-cone written at run time, optional box replication and primordial non-Gaussianity | z = 0 dark-matter P(k) within 2% and reduced bispectrum within 5% on the scales of BAO and redshift-space distortion measurements, three orders of magnitude faster than N-body [src] | Howlett et al. 2015, arXiv:1506.03737 |
| COLA for survey mocks | COLA with 10 steps plus an HOD | 600 WiggleZ mocks; haloes of 10¹² M_⊙; no systematic error in the velocity power spectrum for k < 0.15 h/Mpc and 3% for k < 0.2 h/Mpc [src] | Koda et al. 2015, arXiv:1507.05329 |
| Quick particle mesh (QPM) | low-resolution particle mesh; particles sampled according to local density stand for haloes, then an HOD | mock haloes with N-point statistics "nearly equivalent" to resolved haloes; no accuracy figure in the abstract [src] | White, Tinker & McBride 2013, arXiv:1309.5532 |
| ICE-COLA (optimised COLA) | parameter study against MICE-GC | with 40 linearly spaced steps from z_i about 20 and a force mesh three times finer than the particle grid: matter P(k) within 1% for k ≲ 1 h/Mpc; halo mass function within 5%; halo bias within 2% for k ≲ 0.7 h/Mpc; redshift-space halo monopole and quadrupole within 4% for k ≲ 0.4 h/Mpc; valid for 0 < z < 1 and M > 10¹²·⁵ M_⊙/h [src] | Izard et al. 2015, arXiv:1509.04685 |
| FastPM | plain particle mesh with modified kick and drift factors that enforce the correct linear (1LPT) growth; no force splitting and no separate 2LPT solution | N_s = 10 steps with force-resolution factor B = 2 costs 10 times less than N_s = 40, B = 3 with very similar halo benchmarks at z = 0; lower halo stochasticity than COLA at equal step number [src] | Feng et al. 2016, arXiv:1603.00476 |
| sCOLA, tiled | COLA in space as well as time: the volume is cut into tiles evolved independently, with a buffer region and Dirichlet boundary conditions from LPT | degree of parallelism equal to the number of tiles; "perfectly parallel" [src] | Leclercq et al. 2020, arXiv:2003.04925 |
| sCOLA, original | far field from LPT, near field from the N-body code, the two fully decoupled; a small volume reproduces the result of the same volume embedded in a larger run | proof-of-concept zooms; proposed for tiling and for pencil-beam surveys [src] | Tassev et al. 2015, arXiv:1502.07751 |
| BullFrog | second-order integrator whose steps reproduce 2LPT without computing 2LPT displacements; converges to the exact solution as the step number grows, also after shell crossing | improves on FastPM and COLA at no extra cost; trivial to add to a code that already has FastPM stepping [src] | Rampf et al. 2024, arXiv:2409.19049 |

Three consequences follow for a user of these codes. First, accuracy is a function of (number of steps, force-mesh factor, starting redshift), so "a COLA mock" without these three numbers has no defined accuracy. Second, halo internal structure is not resolved: a 20-step COLA forward model describes the density field around the most massive local clusters but does not give converged virial masses [src] (Stopyra et al. 2023, arXiv:2304.09193), which is why constrained-simulation projects resimulate the inferred initial conditions with a full N-body code (section 9). Third, these codes are in survey production: the DESI N-body comparison included FastPM as a quasi-N-body code planned for some DESI analyses (arXiv:2112.09138), and UNIT ran FastPM boxes beside its full N-body runs (arXiv:1811.02111).

Neutrinos in FastPM: Bayer et al. 2020 (arXiv:2007.13394) add neutrino particles with quasi-random sampling of the Fermi–Dirac distribution and report percent-level CDM and total matter power for M_ν ≲ 0.6 eV at z ≲ 2 in about 25 steps from z = 99, at a cost of 5–20% more run time per step for 1024³ CDM particles [src].

**5.2 LPT plus an effective bias model.** No N-body step at all: a Zel'dovich or 2LPT density field is mapped to tracers with a parametrised, stochastic bias calibrated on a reference.
- EZmocks: Zel'dovich field plus an effective tracer bias model calibrated on the clustering of the data (eBOSS) or of N-body HOD mocks (DESI); reproduces two- and three-point statistics; cost close to a lognormal mock (Zhao et al. 2020, arXiv:2007.08997; DESI set-up in Forero-Sánchez et al. 2024, arXiv:2411.12027).
- CoLoRe-2LPT: 2LPT density and velocities for quasars and Lyα skewers, replacing the earlier lognormal approach (Ruiz-Herrera Bernal et al. 2026, arXiv:2607.27412).
- Calibrated halo-assignment methods (Patchy, Halogen) and predictive Lagrangian methods (Pinocchio, PeakPatch) are the other entries of the comparison project (Blot et al. 2018, arXiv:1806.09497).

The methods of this family and the papers that introduced them (review: Monaco 2016, arXiv:1605.07752, which traces their foundations to the late 1960s–early 1990s):

| Method | Ingredients | Accuracy stated in its abstract | Survey use | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PTHalos | perturbation theory for the large-scale field plus virialised halo profiles, then halo occupation numbers | exact two- and three-point statistics on large scales, "rather accurate" into the non-linear regime; several orders of magnitude faster than N-body [src] | 600 BOSS DR9 CMASS mocks from a 2LPT field with calibrated halo masses, halo clustering recovered to about 10% (Manera et al. 2012, arXiv:1203.6609) [src] | Scoccimarro & Sheth 2001, arXiv:astro-ph/0106120 |
| PINOCCHIO | LPT with ellipsoidal collapse applied to the linear field; gives masses, positions, velocities and merger histories | mass function to better than 10% for haloes of at least 30–50 particles; individual masses to 20–40%; positions to 1–2 Mpc rms [src] | one of the seven methods of the covariance comparison project (arXiv:1806.09477) | Monaco et al. 2001, arXiv:astro-ph/0109323 |
| PATCHY | augmented LPT (2LPT on large scales, spherical collapse on small ones), exponential deterministic bias, stochastic bias with a negative-binomial high-density regime; five parameters calibrated on BigMultiDark | halo power spectra within about 2% up to k about 1 h/Mpc at z = 0.577; neglecting over-dispersion costs 10% at k about 0.4 h/Mpc [src] | 12,288 MultiDark-Patchy light-cones for BOSS DR12 (6000 for DR11), 0.15 < z < 0.75, calibrated on an abundance-matching reference and on the data; data reproduced within 1σ up to k = 0.3 h/Mpc (Kitaura et al. 2015, arXiv:1509.06400) [src] | Kitaura, Yepes & Prada 2013, arXiv:1307.3285 |
| EZmocks | Zel'dovich field plus stochastic, scale-dependent, non-local and non-linear bias | P(k) within 1% up to k = 0.55 h/Mpc and ξ down to r = 10 Mpc/h against N-body; bispectrum within 20%; cost of a lognormal mock [src] | eBOSS, DESI (above) | Chuang et al. 2014, arXiv:1409.1124 |
| HALOGEN | 2LPT field, analytic mass function, one-parameter stochastic bias | two-point function within 2% at 10 < r < 50 Mpc/h and within 15% at about 100 Mpc/h [src] | — | Avila et al. 2014, arXiv:1412.5228 |

The nIFTy comparison (Chuang et al. 2014, arXiv:1412.7729) ran these methods against BigMultiDark: a proper bias model is essential, perturbation-theory methods with a realistic bias agree very well, and only the semi-N-body method reached the 1% level on the quadrupole at r < 25 Mpc/h or k > 0.15 h/Mpc [src].

**5.3 Lognormal and Gaussian fields.** A Gaussian field transformed point by point; exact two-point function by construction, wrong higher orders. The lognormal model of the mass distribution is that of Coles & Jones 1991 (doi:10.1093/mnras/248.1.1; the density distribution is close to lognormal at late times [sec: arXiv:2112.05165, Sect. 9.1]; the paper itself was not read). FLASK (Xavier et al. 2016, arXiv:1602.08503) draws correlated lognormal or Gaussian fields on tomographic shells; its paper shows that the lognormal shape makes some density–convergence correlations unattainable, proposes two remedies and reaches sub-percent accuracy for joint clustering and lensing realisations [src]. GLASS builds a light-cone as nested shells of matter, galaxies and lensing, with transformed Gaussian fields generated to essentially arbitrary precision and an iterative line-of-sight integration for lensing; it reaches percent-level two-point statistics for clustering and weak lensing [src] (Tessore et al. 2023, arXiv:2302.01942).

**5.4 Learned surrogates.** Field-level neural emulators and control-variate estimators that pair a few expensive runs with many cheap ones were read this session but lie outside the source list; see "Not verified".

**Choosing between them.** The rule that the comparison papers support is: use the cheapest method whose error on the statistic you need is below your tolerance, and state the reference it was calibrated on. Methods calibrated on two-point statistics are not validated for the bispectrum (section 5 of `systematics.md`).

## 6. Galaxy–halo connection
<!-- key: galaxy-halo-connection -->

Review: Wechsler & Tinker 2018 (arXiv:1804.03097), which defines the galaxy–halo connection as the multivariate distribution of galaxy and halo properties. Its summary numbers: star-formation efficiency peaks in haloes near 10¹² M_⊙, where less than 20% of the baryons have turned into stars; the intrinsic scatter in stellar mass at fixed halo mass is below 0.2 dex above that mass [src].

**Foundations.**
- Halo model. The non-linear power spectrum is the sum of a term from pairs in different haloes and a term from pairs in one halo (Seljak 2000, arXiv:astro-ph/0001493) [src]; Peacock & Smith 2000 (arXiv:astro-ph/0005010) introduce the halo occupation number, with one galaxy at the halo centre and the others as satellites tracing the halo mass, as a recipe for non-local bias [src]; review: Cooray & Sheth 2002 (arXiv:astro-ph/0206508).
- HOD. Berlind & Weinberg 2001 (arXiv:astro-ph/0109001) define the bias of a galaxy population by the probability P(N|M) that a halo of virial mass M holds N galaxies, plus the spatial and velocity distributions of galaxies within haloes, and show which clustering statistics respond to which part of P(N|M) [src].
- Central–satellite split. In dissipationless simulations the mean occupation of subhaloes is a step, a shoulder and a power-law tail: a step-like central term plus Poisson satellites with ⟨N_s⟩ ∝ M^b, b about 1, the full distribution being sub-Poisson for ⟨N⟩ < 4 (Kravtsov et al. 2003, arXiv:astro-ph/0308519) [src]. An SPH simulation and a semi-analytic model give the same form for galaxies, with M₁ ≈ 14 M_min (SPH) or 18 M_min (semi-analytic) between the minimum mass for a central and the mass hosting one satellite on average (Zheng et al. 2004, arXiv:astro-ph/0408564) [src]. The five-parameter form used today comes from this paper, and satellites are usually placed on an NFW profile following Zheng, Coil & Zehavi 2007 (arXiv:astro-ph/0703457) [sec: arXiv:2112.05165, Sect. 9.9.1].
- Abundance matching. Vale & Ostriker 2004 (arXiv:astro-ph/0402500) replace linearity between light and mass by a monotonic relation between galaxy luminosity and halo or subhalo mass [src]. Conroy, Wechsler & Kravtsov 2005 (arXiv:astro-ph/0512234) match luminosity to the maximum circular velocity, taken at the time of accretion for subhaloes, and reproduce the SDSS and DEEP2 correlation functions over 0.1 < r_p < 10 Mpc/h, and Lyman-break galaxies at z about 3–5 [src]. Current implementations prefer the peak circular velocity over the history of the subhalo and add scatter; the method needs subhaloes followed over many dynamical times, and "orphan" galaxies whose subhalo has fallen below the resolution must be modelled [sec: arXiv:2112.05165, Sect. 9.9.2].
- Assembly bias. Gao, Springel & White 2005 (arXiv:astro-ph/0506510): below about 10¹³ M_⊙/h the large-scale clustering of haloes depends on formation time; at 10¹¹ M_⊙/h the oldest 10% are more than 5 times more strongly correlated than the youngest 10%, against the assumption of HOD models [src]. Wechsler et al. 2005 (arXiv:astro-ph/0512416): clustering depends on concentration, with a sign change at the non-linear mass M*, and on subhalo occupation [src]. Croton, Gao & White 2006 (arXiv:astro-ph/0605636) name the effect and measure it on galaxies by shuffling a semi-analytic population among haloes of equal mass in the Millennium simulation: two-point correlations rise by 10% for galaxies brighter than M_bJ − 5 log h = −17 and fall by a similar amount for those brighter than −20; shuffling at fixed formation redshift or concentration does not remove it [src].
- Occupation models with assembly bias. The decorated HOD of Hearin et al. 2015 (arXiv:1512.03050), in the Halotools package: for SDSS-like samples assembly bias can change clustering by up to a factor of two at r about 200 kpc and by about 15% in the linear regime, in either direction on large scales [src].

The methods used to populate gravity-only simulations, from fewest to most assumptions about galaxy formation:

| Method | What is assumed | Where it is used in the sources read | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Halo occupation distribution (HOD) | the number of galaxies in a halo depends on halo mass only; a step-like central term plus a power-law satellite term (the "vanilla" five-parameter form) | DESI AbacusSummit mocks; Flagship 2 (with abundance matching); all DESI HOD-systematics tests | Mena-Fernández et al. 2024, arXiv:2404.03008; arXiv:2405.13495 |
| Extended HOD | adds velocity bias, incompleteness, a satellite-profile parameter and an assembly-bias parameter (occupation depends on a secondary halo property such as concentration or environment) | DESI "baseline" and "extended" LRG models | arXiv:2404.03008 |
| Subhalo abundance matching (SHAM) | galaxies are rank-ordered onto subhaloes by a mass or velocity proxy, with scatter; needs resolved subhaloes | DESI DR2 reference mocks on Uchuu (read, outside the source list); Flagship 2 luminosities | arXiv:2405.13495 |
| Semi-analytic models | simplified galaxy-formation physics run on halo merger trees | Millennium; Last Journey; MillenniumTNG (calibrated on its own hydrodynamical run); SIBELIUS-DARK with GALFORM | arXiv:astro-ph/0504097; arXiv:2006.01697; arXiv:2210.10059; arXiv:2202.04099 |
| Hydrodynamical simulation as the galaxy model | everything in section 3 | IllustrisTNG galaxy clustering agrees with SDSS at z about 0.1 as a function of stellar mass and colour [src] | arXiv:1707.03397 |
| Effective bias on an approximate field | no haloes at all; a stochastic, non-local bias fitted to a reference | EZmocks, CoLoRe | arXiv:2007.08997; arXiv:2607.27412 |

**Assembly bias.** Halo clustering depends on properties other than mass (formation time, concentration, environment); if galaxy occupation correlates with those properties, the mass-only HOD is wrong. The test on a hydrodynamical simulation that is most often cited: in TNG300 the mass-only HOD underpredicts the real-space galaxy correlation function by about 15% on 1 < r < 20 Mpc/h; the secondary properties that best repair it are the local environment, the velocity-dispersion anisotropy and σ²R_halfmass, ahead of spin, formation epoch and concentration [src] (Hadzhiyska et al. 2019, arXiv:1911.02610).

**Why this matters for cosmology.** The DESI DR1 BAO analysis detects an HOD-dependent shift of 0.19% in α_AP in configuration space at 3.3σ and none at 3σ elsewhere [src] (arXiv:2404.03008). The DESI DR1 full-shape analysis finds that parameter shifts between HOD models often exceed 20% of the statistical error, and adds a systematic covariance term C_HOD to the data covariance rather than to the posteriors [src] (Findlay et al. 2024, arXiv:2411.12023). Analyses beyond two-point statistics rely on one simulation suite and one HOD family, a point developed in `cosmo-beyond-two-point`.

**Resolution requirement.** Halo statistics are converged at 1% only above 500 particles per halo [src] (arXiv:1804.05865). HalfDome resolves haloes only above about 6 × 10¹² M_⊙/h [src] (arXiv:2407.17462): enough for LRG-like hosts, not for emission-line galaxies. Flagship 2 was sized to resolve 10¹¹ M_⊙/h haloes with m_p = 10⁹ M_⊙/h, that is, with about 100 particles [der: 10¹¹/10⁹ from arXiv:2405.13495 section 2.1], and in practice goes far below that: haloes are kept down to 10 particles, because the Euclid flux limit requires host masses of about 2 × 10¹⁰ M_⊙/h, that is 20 particles; the halo mass function is incomplete below 10¹¹·⁵ M_⊙/h (about 300 particles), so masses below that are reassigned by abundance matching to a function with the low-mass slope of Tinker et al. 2008 normalised to the Flagship abundance at 10¹¹·⁵ M_⊙/h, and then smeared between integer particle numbers with a local power law; the paper argues that two-point clustering is unaffected because halo bias is nearly independent of mass at low mass [src: Sect. 4.2 and Eqs. 3–5 of arXiv:2405.13495]. The faintest Flagship galaxies therefore sit in haloes that no convergence statement covers.

## 7. Light-cones and survey realism
<!-- key: light-cones-and-survey-realism -->

A survey observes the past light-cone, not a snapshot. Four constructions are in use.

1. **Particle light-cone written on the fly.** Each particle is output when the shrinking light surface of the observer sweeps past it. Flagship 2 does this over the full sky to z = 3 by replicating the 3600 Mpc/h box, giving 31 trillion particle outputs and lensing maps at HEALPix N_side = 8192 (0.43 arcmin pixels) [src] (arXiv:2405.13495, section 2.1). FLAMINGO writes light-cones on the fly for up to 8 observers [src] (arXiv:2306.04024); Last Journey writes particle light-cones in situ from z about 10 [src] (arXiv:2006.01697); MillenniumTNG and AbacusSummit provide direct light-cone outputs (arXiv:2210.10059, arXiv:2110.11398); HalfDome provides full-sky light-cones for z = 0–4 [src] (arXiv:2407.17462).
2. **Pseudo-light-cone from snapshots.** Boxes at a few redshifts are cut to the survey geometry and concatenated. The DESI DR1 EZmocks are of this kind, and the paper states that mocks with actual redshift evolution are not used [src] (arXiv:2411.12027, section 2.1).
3. **Nested shells.** GLASS draws matter shells in sequence, correlating each with the previous ones, and integrates along the line of sight for the lensing fields (arXiv:2302.01942).
4. **Light-cone from an approximate dynamical model during inference.** BORG applied to Quaia includes light-cone effects, redshift-space distortions, bias and selection in an LPT forward model (title and abstract read; outside the source list).

**Foundations.**
- On-the-fly light-cones were used in the Hubble Volume simulations [sec: arXiv:2112.05165, Sect. 9.6]: giga-particle runs mapped onto past light-cones to z about 1.4 over 10,000 deg² and to z about 0.5 over two full spheres (Evrard et al. 2001, arXiv:astro-ph/0110246) [src].
- Snapshot-based light-cones with replication: MoMaF (Blaizot et al. 2003, arXiv:astro-ph/0309305) quantifies its own artefacts: replication biases the clustering signal low, typically by less than 10% around the correlation length, and finite volume removes the signal on scales above about a tenth of the box side [src]. Kitzbichler & White 2006 (arXiv:astro-ph/0609636) build deep light-cones through the Millennium simulation [src], with viewing angles chosen to avoid repeating structure along the line of sight [sec: arXiv:2112.05165, Sect. 9.6].
- Interpolation between snapshots: Merson et al. 2012 (arXiv:1206.4049) place each galaxy at the epoch at which it enters the observer's light-cone, by interpolation between outputs, with galaxy properties evolving along the cone [src]. The review gives 50–100 snapshots as the number needed for accurate results, and notes that interpolating particles underestimates the central densities of haloes and that an object can appear twice or never [sec: arXiv:2112.05165, Sect. 9.6].
- Shells for lensing: the "onion universe" of Fosalba et al. 2007 (arXiv:0711.1540) compresses the light-cone output of a 2048³ GADGET-2 run into concentric angular maps and builds all-sky convergence maps from them; sampling variance biases the convergence variance by 25% at 10 arcmin on patches of a few degrees [src]. The MICE Grand Challenge run that followed has about 70 billion particles in (3 Gpc/h)³ (Fosalba et al. 2013, arXiv:1312.1707) [src]; it is the reference against which ICE-COLA was tuned (section 5).

**Box replication.** Replicating a box to fill a light-cone repeats structure and removes modes larger than the box. The DESI EZmocks avoid it with (6 Gpc/h)³ boxes [src] (arXiv:2411.12027); Flagship 2 replicates [src] (arXiv:2405.13495); the size of the effect on lensing statistics is the subject of arXiv:2511.20423 (numbers not read).

**Survey realism.** The layers added on top of a light-cone, each a possible source of mismatch between mocks and data:
- angular footprint and radial selection n(z);
- fibre assignment: the DESI mocks use a fast emulator of the assignment, whose shot-noise properties differ from the real algorithm [src] (arXiv:2411.12027; the difference is noted in the semi-analytic covariance paper, read outside the source list);
- photometric and spectroscopic systematics, included in the eBOSS EZmocks [src] (arXiv:2007.08997);
- galaxy properties: Flagship 2 assigns luminosities, colours, spectral energy distributions, shapes, sizes, intrinsic alignments, emission lines and lensing to each galaxy (arXiv:2405.13495);
- distance-indicator errors: the DESI-PV mocks mimic Fundamental Plane, Tully–Fisher and SN Ia measurements with the statistical properties of the data [src] (arXiv:2512.03228);
- quasar continua, high-column-density systems and metals for the Lyα forest (arXiv:1912.02763, arXiv:2607.27412).

The DESI DR1 clustering of data and simulations agrees to within 2% in the inferred real-space overdensity field according to the DESI 2024 II abstract (read, outside the source list); the instrument and survey side of realism is in `cosmo-surveys-facilities` and `cosmo-galaxy-clustering`.

## 8. Emulators
<!-- key: emulators -->

An emulator interpolates a summary statistic between simulations run on a design in parameter space. The design choices that decide its accuracy:

- **What is emulated.** The non-linear correction to the linear matter power spectrum (EuclidEmulator2, arXiv:2010.11288) or the spectrum itself (Mira-Titan IV, arXiv:2207.12345); baryon emulators predict the ratio to gravity-only (arXiv:2011.15018, arXiv:2410.17109); the Lyα emulators predict P1D as a function of the local linear amplitude and slope (section 4).
- **Design.** Latin hypercubes (PRIYA, arXiv:2306.05471; sliced maximin Latin hypercube in Dark Quest, arXiv:1811.09504); a nested design that can be refined from 26 to 111 models (Mira-Titan, arXiv:1508.02654, arXiv:2207.12345); cosmology rescaling of a few boxes to 800 points (BACCO, arXiv:2004.06245).
- **Variance control.** Fixed-and-paired initial conditions (EuclidEmulator2, ForestFlow); Zel'dovich control variates (Aemulus ν, arXiv:2303.09762).
- **Multi-fidelity.** A few high-resolution runs corrected by many cheap ones: Mira-Titan IV (111 high-resolution, 1776 low-resolution, plus perturbation theory on large scales), PRIYA (3 high- and 48 low-fidelity).
- **Interpolator.** Gaussian processes (Dark Emulator, PRIYA, Lyssa, FLAMINGO response) or neural networks (BACCO baryons, LaCE; DarkEmulator2, read outside the source list).
- **Resolution correction.** EuclidEmulator2 ships a post-processing correction for residual resolution effects (arXiv:2010.11288).

**Foundations.**
- Fitting functions came first. HALOFIT (Smith et al. 2002, arXiv:astro-ph/0207664) fits a halo-model-inspired form to a library of scale-free and CDM simulations, after showing that stable clustering fails [src]; its revision on 16 higher-resolution models is accurate to 5% for k ≤ 1 h/Mpc at 0 ≤ z ≤ 10 and 10% for 1 ≤ k ≤ 10 h/Mpc at 0 ≤ z ≤ 3 (Takahashi et al. 2012, arXiv:1208.2701) [src]. HMcode (section 3) is the current member of this family.
- The emulator idea: "cosmic calibration" (Heitmann et al. 2006, arXiv:astro-ph/0606154) is the statistical method for combining a limited set of simulations with data and characterising the uncertainty [src].
- The Coyote Universe established the practice. Paper I (Heitmann et al. 2008, arXiv:0812.1052): the non-linear matter P(k) can be simulated to 1% out to k about 1 h/Mpc given precise initial conditions, very large volumes, sufficient mass resolution and accurate time stepping [src]. Paper II (Heitmann et al. 2009, arXiv:0902.0429): 37 wCDM models suffice over a prior range set by the CMB [src]. Paper III (Lawrence et al. 2009, arXiv:0912.4490): nearly 1000 runs over 38 cosmologies and a public emulator accurate at the percent level to k about 1 h/Mpc [src].
- Mira-Titan II (Lawrence et al. 2017, arXiv:1705.03388) extended the scheme to eight parameters, with massive neutrinos and dynamical dark energy, from 36 models: about 4% to k about 5 Mpc⁻¹ and z ≤ 2, with about 1% announced once more than a hundred models are run [src]; paper IV, with 111 models, quotes 2–3% (`results.md` §2).

The accuracy table is in `results.md` §2. Two rules for reading it. The quoted figure is measured against held-out simulations of the same suite, so it is an interpolation error; the simulation error (section 1 of `results.md`) and the baryonic effect add to it. And the range of validity in parameters matters as much as the range in k: w_a, neutrino mass and curvature are covered by some emulators and not by others, and the EuclidEmulator2 paper itself reports that adding w_a "significantly increases the complexity and expense" [src] (arXiv:2010.11288). How emulator error enters a likelihood is treated in `cosmo-statistics-inference`.

## 9. Constrained simulations and reconstructions of the local Universe
<!-- key: constrained-simulations-and-reconstructions-of -->

A constrained simulation is a realisation of the initial conditions chosen so that the evolved field reproduces an observed region. Unconstrained simulations can be compared with the data only statistically; constrained ones allow object-by-object comparison (named clusters, the Local Group, a void). Two families of methods exist, and they differ in what is treated as data and in what is sampled.

**9.1 Constrained realisations from peculiar velocities (linear, Gaussian).** A Wiener filter gives the mean linear field given sparse, noisy radial velocities, and the Hoffman–Ribak algorithm adds a random residual with the correct covariance, giving initial conditions that are then evolved with an N-body code. CLUES built on Cosmicflows-2 is the reference application: the cell-to-cell scatter between constrained runs is 2 to 3 times smaller than between random runs on a 5 Mpc/h scale in the inner region; the scatter between simulated and observation-reconstructed velocity fields is 104 ± 4 km/s; the simulations resemble the local Universe out to 150 Mpc/h on scales of a few tens of megaparsecs [src] (Sorce et al. 2015, arXiv:1510.04900). The constraints are linear, so structure below the constraint scale is a random draw from the prior.

Foundations of this family. The Wiener filter for large-scale-structure reconstruction is the linear minimum-variance solution given the data and a prior covariance; for a Gaussian field it coincides with the maximum of the Bayesian posterior, it predicts the field in unsampled regions, and its mean field can be supplemented with constrained realisations (Zaroubi et al. 1994, arXiv:astro-ph/9410080) [src]. The Hoffman–Ribak algorithm (Hoffman & Ribak 1991, doi:10.1086/186160) turns an unconstrained Gaussian realisation φ into one that satisfies M linear constraints with kernels H_j and values č_j: φ̌(k) = φ(k) + P(k) H_i(k) ξ_ij⁻¹ (č_j − c_j), where c_j are the values of the constraints in the unconstrained field and ξ_ij their covariance [sec: Eq. 88 of arXiv:2112.05165; the 1991 paper itself was not read]. It does not account for the displacement between Lagrangian and Eulerian positions, and scales that have shell-crossed are hard to constrain, so that projects rerun the unconstrained phases until a wanted outcome appears [sec: arXiv:2112.05165, Sect. 6.3.7]. The CLUES technique is reviewed by Gottlöber, Hoffman & Yepes 2010 (arXiv:1005.2687) [src].

**9.2 Bayesian forward modelling of the initial conditions (field-level inference).** The parameter space is the three-dimensional initial density field; a structure-formation model maps it to the evolved field; a data model (bias, selection, noise) links that to the observations; the posterior is sampled with Hamiltonian Monte Carlo.

**9.3 From an inferred field to a simulation.** The sequence used by the current projects is: (i) inference at a few Mpc/h with an approximate solver; (ii) draw posterior samples of the initial conditions; (iii) add unconstrained small-scale modes; (iv) resimulate with a full N-body or hydrodynamical code, possibly as a zoom with multi-scale initial conditions (MUSIC, arXiv:1103.6031). The output is an ensemble; a single constrained run is one posterior sample and carries no error bar.

**9.4 Validation.** Posterior-predictive tests on the power spectrum, bispectrum, Gaussianity of the initial conditions and halo mass function (Manticore, arXiv:2505.10682, arXiv:2606.10020); comparison with data not used in the inference: direct distance tracers scored by Bayesian evidence, residual redshifts, velocity scaling and the need for an external bulk flow (Stiskalek et al. 2025, arXiv:2502.00121); CMB lensing and kSZ stacking (arXiv:2606.10020); matched counterparts of named clusters (arXiv:2505.10682, arXiv:2602.03699).

**9.5 What it is not.** The reconstructions assume a cosmology in the prior and in the forward model. A BORG field is a conditional reconstruction, not a parameter constraint; the distinction between sampled initial conditions, Gaussian field-level likelihoods (flip) and neural approaches is drawn in `cosmo-beyond-two-point`, and the use of reconstructed velocity fields for peculiar-velocity corrections in `cosmo-peculiar-velocities`.

**Massive neutrinos: the methods and their papers.**

| Particles in a fast particle-mesh code | quasi-random sampling of the Fermi–Dirac distribution in FastPM | percent-level CDM and total matter power for M_ν ≲ 0.6 eV at z ≲ 2, in about 25 steps from z = 99 [src] | Bayer et al. 2020, arXiv:2007.13394 |

A consequence for mocks: in massive-neutrino cosmologies the halo bias must be defined with respect to the cold matter, not the total matter; the wrong definition biases the growth rate from anisotropic clustering by 1–2% (DEMNUni; Castorina et al. 2015, arXiv:1505.07148) [src].

Three implementations appear in the suites read:
- neutrinos as a second particle species, accurate up to the largest mass simulated (0.5 eV in Aemulus ν, arXiv:2303.09762);
- the δf method in SWIFT, used by FLAMINGO: particles follow the full non-linear neutrino phase-space distribution but are statistically weighted against the background distribution, which minimises shot noise and removes the spurious early back-reaction on cold matter; the weighting is applied in the long-range mesh force only. The FLAMINGO baseline has one massive species of 0.06 eV and two massless ones [src] (arXiv:2306.04024, section 2.2; arXiv:2305.13380);
- linear neutrino perturbations added to the force as a source term or look-up table in the N-body gauge (EuclidEmulator2, arXiv:2010.11288; Flagship 2 with the minimal mass of 0.0587 eV, arXiv:2405.13495; GINKAKU, arXiv:2605.28581);
- particle neutrinos in a fast particle-mesh code (FastPM, arXiv:2007.13394).

The Euclid code comparison tested eleven full N-body implementations (not all independent), two schemes with approximate time integration and four codes that predict or emulate P(k), on common initial data; it finds them fully consistent and concludes that the effect of massive neutrinos is modelled at the sub-percent level in the most common summary statistics (power spectrum, bispectrum, halo mass function, halo bias) [src] (Adamek et al. 2022, arXiv:2211.12457).

**Modified gravity.** N-body codes for f(R), DGP and symmetron models solve a non-linear scalar-field equation for the fifth force, including its screening. The f(R) model simulated by almost all of them is that of Hu & Sawicki 2007 (arXiv:0705.1158), whose paper states that the viability of field amplitudes |f_R| > 10⁻⁶ rests on the structure of the galactic halo and therefore requires simulations [src]. The first such simulation (Oyaizu 2008, arXiv:0807.2449) reduces the problem to a non-linear Poisson equation for the scalar degree of freedom, solved by Newton–Gauss–Seidel relaxation with multigrid acceleration, and finds the matter power enhanced by about 20% for large allowed modifications [src]. Schmidt 2009 (arXiv:0905.0858) does the same for the brane-bending mode of DGP, in the quasi-static approximation [src]. The production codes extend standard ones: ECOSMOG (Li et al. 2011, arXiv:1110.1379) and ISIS (Llinares, Mota & Winther 2013, arXiv:1307.6748) on RAMSES with adaptive-mesh multigrid solvers, and MG-GADGET (Puchwein, Baldi & Springel 2013, arXiv:1305.2418), which runs with hydrodynamics and shows the degeneracy between AGN feedback and modified gravity in the matter power spectrum [src]. Sizes of the effect, from the review: DGP raises P(k) almost scale-independently, by about 15% (3%) for H₀r_c = 1 (5) at z = 0; f(R) raises it scale-dependently, by about 25% (5%) at k about 10 h/Mpc for f_R0 = 10⁻⁵ (10⁻⁶) [sec: arXiv:2112.05165, Sect. 7.10]. The code-comparison project finds that the fractional deviation of the matter power spectrum from ΛCDM agrees between codes to better than 1% up to k about 5–10 h/Mpc, with good agreement on the velocity-divergence spectrum, halo abundances and profiles, and that the quasi-static approximation is very good for the observables considered, tested on the symmetron model [src] (Winther et al. 2015, arXiv:1506.06384). The statement is about the ratio to ΛCDM, not about the absolute spectrum. The Euclid collaboration repeated a comparison for a wider set of models in 2024 (title and abstract read, outside the source list). Model definitions and constraints are in `cosmo-dark-energy-modified-gravity`.

**Non-cold dark matter.** Warm dark matter runs are part of Sherwood (arXiv:1605.03462); fuzzy and self-interacting dark matter need dedicated solvers and are covered, with the limits they support, in `cosmo-neutrinos-dark-matter`.

## 11. Halo finding and halo definitions
<!-- key: halo-finding-and-halo-definitions -->

A halo catalogue is the product of an operational definition, and the mass of a halo depends on it. The comparison of 17 finders found very good agreement on the structures recovered and on their internal properties, while the mass returned varied greatly with the definition [sec: arXiv:2112.05165, Sect. 9.4.1]. Three families are in use.

| Family | Definition | Paper | What its abstract or the review states |
|---|---|---|---|
| Friends-of-friends (FoF) | particles closer than a linking length b, in units of the mean inter-particle separation, are joined; the group is bounded by an isodensity surface | Davis et al. 1985, doi:10.1086/163168 (with Press & Davis 1982, no identifier here) | the abstract describes cold-dark-matter simulations and their numerical methods and does not name the algorithm [src]; the attribution of FoF to it is the review's [sec: arXiv:2112.05165, Sect. 9.4.1] |
| FoF, what it selects | the boundary is at a density of 81.62 times the mean for b = 0.2; the enclosed overdensity depends on the concentration, hence on mass, and is not the often-quoted 180; FoF masses depend on mass resolution, and a percolation-based correction fails on real haloes because of substructure | More et al. 2011, arXiv:1103.0005 | [src] |
| Spherical overdensity (SO) | a sphere whose mean enclosed density equals a threshold: the spherical-collapse value, 200 times the mean density, or 200 times the critical density | Lacey & Cole 1994, arXiv:astro-ph/9402069; threshold of Bryan & Norman 1997, arXiv:astro-ph/9710107 | Lacey & Cole select haloes at mean overdensities of 100–200 and find the Press–Schechter mass function accurate to better than 30% over a factor 1000 in mass [src]; the use of the Bryan & Norman value as the SO threshold is the review's statement [sec: arXiv:2112.05165, Sect. 9.4.1] |
| Subhaloes in configuration space (SUBFIND) | locally overdense, gravitationally bound groups inside an FoF halo | Springel et al. 2000, arXiv:astro-ph/0012055 | the abstract presents "new parallel N-body and substructure identification codes" for a cluster with about 2 × 10⁷ particles inside the virial radius [src]; the name SUBFIND and its inclusion in the GADGET-4 release are from the review [sec: arXiv:2112.05165, Sect. 9.4.2] |
| Phase-space FoF (ROCKSTAR) | adaptive hierarchical refinement of FoF groups in six phase-space dimensions and one time dimension; unbound particles removed | Behroozi et al. 2011, arXiv:1110.4372 | 10 CPU hours and 60 GB per billion particles; better substructure recovery than other finders; halo cores are not at rest with respect to the halo bulk, with offsets up to 350 km/s in massive clusters at z = 0 [src] |
| Competitive SO (CompaSO) | SO with a tidal-radius criterion before particles are assigned competitively to haloes; run on the fly | Hadzhiyska et al. 2021, arXiv:2110.11408 | the finder of AbacusSummit; compared with ROCKSTAR on radius–mass relations and two-point functions; a cleaning method is described in a companion paper [src] |

**Comparison project.** Knebe et al. 2011 (arXiv:1104.0949) ran FoF, SO and phase-space finders on common mock haloes and on one cosmological simulation: all recovered positions and the maximum of the circular-velocity curve; configuration-space finders lost substructure near the host centre; most finders could not recover substructure with fewer than 30–40 particles, phase-space finders reached 10–20; on the cosmological volume the finders agreed "remarkably well" on mass, position, velocity and peak of the rotation curve [src].

**Halo mass function and profile.**
- Press & Schechter 1974 (doi:10.1086/152650; not read here) is the model whose kind of universality Jenkins et al. 2000 (arXiv:astro-ph/0005260) found in simulations: for a suitable halo definition the mass function is almost independent of epoch, cosmological parameters and initial spectrum, with a systematic uncertainty below 10–30% depending on the halo definition, and with a shape that differs from the Press–Schechter one [src].
- Warren et al. 2005 (arXiv:astro-ph/0506395): sixteen 1024³ nested-volume runs over five orders of magnitude in mass; a systematic error of the FoF finder is found and corrected [src]. The review adds that the abundance of haloes with fewer than about 100 particles is overestimated, and that the correction is not universal [sec: arXiv:2112.05165, Sects. 8.1 and 9.4.1].
- Tinker et al. 2008 (arXiv:0803.2706): SO mass function calibrated to ≲ 5% at z = 0 for 10¹¹ < M < 10¹⁵ M_⊙/h, for a range of overdensities; it is not universal at that accuracy, the amplitude falling by 20–50% from z = 0 to 2.5 depending on the mass definition [src]. This is the reason a halo mass must always be quoted with its definition (M_200m, M_200c, M_vir, M_FoF).
- Navarro, Frenk & White 1996 (arXiv:astro-ph/9611107): equilibrium density profiles have the same shape whatever the halo mass, initial spectrum and cosmology, fitted over two decades in radius by a two-parameter formula; low-mass haloes are denser, reflecting their earlier collapse [src]. HOD satellites are usually placed on this profile [sec: arXiv:2112.05165, Sect. 9.9.1].

**What it changes for a mock.** Because the mass of a halo varies with the finder, occupation parameters written in terms of halo mass are tied to the catalogue they were fitted on [der: from the finder dependence of masses stated above]; subhalo abundance matching needs a finder that tracks substructure (ROCKSTAR, SUBFIND or a tracking finder), and its resolution requirement is stricter than that of an HOD [sec: arXiv:2112.05165, Sect. 9.9.2]. Flagship 2 uses ROCKSTAR (`surveys-and-data.md` §4), AbacusSummit CompaSO.
