# Pipeline validation — cosmo-statistics-inference dossier

Scope: how an inference pipeline is validated on synthetic data before it meets the real data —
recovery of a noiseless input, comparison of two independent codes, contaminated data vectors and
their pass criteria, stacked and per-mock tests on noisy realisations, sampler cross-checks,
calibration diagnostics applied to an explicit likelihood, and the noise calibration of shifts
between analysis variants; the scale-cut recipes of each probe, the projection-effect values and
the blinding checklists are in the reference files and sibling dossiers.
Snapshot: 2026-10-19.
From: KiDS-1000 methodology (Joachimi et al. 2021), Sect. 7.

Prerequisites at L2: the projection-effect mechanism, the noiseless-vector test and the DES Y6 pulls
in `physics-and-methods.md#diagnostics-and-mitigations-in-use` and
`results.md#projection-prior-volume-effects-documented`; covariance validation in
`deep/covariance-estimation.md`; sampler validation in
`cosmo-software-ecosystem/deep/samplers-frameworks.md`; simulation-based-inference calibration in
`physics-and-methods.md#validation-tests` and `cosmo-beyond-two-point/deep/simulation-based-inference.md`;
probe-specific scale cuts in `cosmo-weak-lensing/deep/baryonic-feedback.md`,
`cosmo-weak-lensing/deep/ggl-3x2pt.md` and `cosmo-galaxy-clustering/deep/full-shape-modelling.md`;
unblinding lists in `cosmo-galaxy-clustering/deep/blinding.md` and
`cosmo-weak-lensing/deep/blinding-consistency.md`. Equation numbers are those of the paper cited next
to them.

Symbols. MAP the maximum a posteriori point over the full parameter space; M-HPD the marginal
highest-posterior-density interval; PJ-HPD the projected joint HPD interval of KiDS-1000;
`σ_2D` the 2D marginal width used by DES; `α_∥`, `α_⊥` the BAO dilation parameters; TARP the test of
accuracy with random points; PIT the probability integral transform.

## How it is done
<!-- key: how-it-is-done -->

### Step 1. Recover a noiseless input with the full pipeline
<!-- key: step-1-recover-a-noiseless-input-with-the-full -->

- Build the synthetic data vector "from the same modelling pipeline as used in the inference"; the
  absence of noise "guarantees that the likelihood peaks exactly at the parameter combination chosen
  to generate the data vector" (KiDS-1000 methodology, arXiv:2007.01844) [src: Sect. 7]. Centre the
  fiducial nuisance values on the peaks of their informative priors, so that the posterior also
  peaks at the input [src: same].
- Judge recovery with the MAP, not with the marginal: marginal posteriors are not expected to peak at
  the input [src: same]. KiDS-1000 recovers all shown parameters with the MAP "to better than 1.7%"
  [src: Sect. 7, Fig. 21]. Its rule for intervals: M-HPD to compare with earlier measurements,
  PJ-HPD "to assess the recovery of the input S_8" [src: Sect. 7].
- Intervals are read from a histogram of the samples smoothed by a Gaussian kernel with Silverman's
  bandwidth [src: same].
- DES Y3 runs the same test and shows why the MAP is needed: marginalising over the neutrino mass
  shifts the 1D marginal mean of Ω_m and S_8 "by nearly 1σ, while MAP closely recovers the input";
  the shift decreases with constraining power and is "not a systematic bias" (Krause et al. 2021,
  arXiv:2105.13548) [src: Sect. IV.1, Fig. 2]. DES Y3 therefore reports `mean (+upper 34%/−lower 34%)
  (MAP value)` [src: Eq. 36].

### Step 2. Run two independent implementations against each other
<!-- key: step-2-run-two-independent-implementations -->

DES Y3 keeps two complete pipelines, CosmoSIS and CosmoLike, with different Boltzmann codes (CAMB or
CLASS) and different "structure, interpolation and integration routines" [src: arXiv:2105.13548,
Sect. IV.1].

1. Compare the two model vectors at the fiducial point; iterate until `Δχ² < 0.2` ("after multiple
   iterations") [src: same].
2. Cross-fit: each code analyses a data vector generated by the other, in the full cosmological and
   nuisance space; the posteriors must agree [src: same, Fig. 1].
3. Use the residual as the noise floor of all later model tests: a model variation that changes the
   data vector by `Δχ² < 0.2` without refitting is "insignificant as the change in Δχ² is less than
   the residual between the two analysis codes" [src: Sect. V].

### Step 3. Contaminated data vectors: pass criteria
<!-- key: step-3-contaminated-data-vectors-pass-criteria -->

DES Y3 (arXiv:2105.13548) [src: Sect. V]:

1. Two classes of test: known but unmodelled effects (baryons, non-linear bias), mitigated by scale
   cuts; modelled effects with imperfect parameterisations, stress-tested by generating the data
   with an alternative parameterisation.
2. Contaminate "at the upper limit of credible severity for each effect", and combine the unmodelled
   effects in one vector.
3. Fit cosmic shear, 2×2pt, 3×2pt and 3×2pt + Planck, in ΛCDM and wCDM; measure the bias in the
   (Ω_m, S_8) or (Ω_m, w) plane.
4. Pass: 2D bias `< 0.3 σ_2D`, chosen "to ensure that the total potential systematic bias is well below
   1σ statistical uncertainty", and residual `Δχ² < 1` after refitting the baseline model, "in order to
   not bias the goodness of fit".
5. Shortcut: if a variation changes the data vector by `Δχ² < 1` before refitting, the `Δχ²` criterion
   is met automatically and the bias is estimated by importance sampling of the baseline chain.
6. Measure the bias against the 2D marginal of an otherwise identical fit to the uncontaminated vector,
   which "is subject to the same projection effects", not against the input values; the MAP was too
   noisy for this in degenerate models, even "with repeated numerical optimization using the
   Nelder–Mead method".
7. Check that the projection effects are stable against noise with 100 noisy data vectors drawn
   from the data covariance.

KiDS-1000 builds data vectors "that systematically deviate from the ideal model" for each unmodelled
effect, discussed with that effect [src: arXiv:2007.01844, Sect. 7]; its stated outcome for known
modelling and inference systematics is a bias of at most 0.1σ in S_8 (L2,
`results.md#numbers-stated-in-the-papers`). The scale cuts that follow from such tests are in the
weak-lensing dossiers.

### Step 4. Noisy realisations: stacked bias and per-mock pulls
<!-- key: step-4-noisy-realisations-stacked-bias-and-per -->

KiDS-1000 adds "a zero-mean multivariate Gaussian noise realisation drawn with the full covariance" to
the synthetic vector and repeats the validation on ensembles; "our conclusions [are] identical to the
noiseless case" [src: arXiv:2007.01844, Sect. 7].

With full mock catalogues, the DESI DR2 Lyα forest BAO validation (Casas et al. 2025, arXiv:2503.14741)
runs two complementary tests [src: Sects. IV.1–IV.2]:

1. **Stack for bias.** Combine the correlation functions of all mocks (100 Saclay and 300 CoLoRe-QL)
   to very high signal-to-noise and fit; requirement: recover `α_⊥ = α_∥ = 1` "within a third of the
   statistical uncertainty from DESI DR2"; biases above it "must be investigated, corrected, or
   incorporated into the systematic errors" [src: Sect. IV.1].
2. **Separate the boxes from the pipeline.** Repeat on the raw mocks without noise, contaminants or
   continuum fitting; unbiased raw results "rule out intrinsic issues with the simulated boxes", so the
   residual bias belongs to the analysis — here to the way redshift errors were added before the
   continuum fit [src: Sect. IV.1].
3. **Fit each mock.** Look at the scatter of best fits, at the χ² distribution against the expected one
   for the degrees of freedom, and at the pulls `Δα/σ_α` against a unit normal [src: Sect. IV.2,
   Figs. 7–8]. Normal pulls show that the uncertainties are "accurate and approximately Gaussian".
4. **Attribute a χ² excess.** The mock χ² values are higher than expected; Monte Carlo realisations
   started from the best-fit model or from the stacked measurement show that this "arises from the
   model's difficulty in fitting the correlation functions", "not related to the covariance matrix"
   (the DR1 study cited there) [src: Sect. IV.2].
5. **Compare mock errors with data errors.** Mocks without non-linear broadening of the BAO peak
   (Saclay) give errors 10–20% smaller than the data; mocks that include some of it (CoLoRe-QL) agree
   better [src: Sect. IV.2, Fig. 9]. A mock set whose error distribution misses the data's cannot
   validate the data's error bar.

### Step 5. Cross-check the sampler on the final likelihood
<!-- key: step-5-cross-check-the-sampler-on-the-final -->

The DES Y3 + KiDS-1000 joint analysis compares MultiNest, PolyChord, Metropolis–Hastings and emcee on
DES Y3 cosmic shear (arXiv:2305.17173) [src: App. D]: MultiNest underestimates the width of the
68% and 95% S_8 intervals by 12% and 15%; PolyChord matches the MCMC samplers "within a few percent".
Settings: PolyChord `n_live = 500`, `n_repeats = 60`, tolerance 0.01; MultiNest `n_live = 1000`,
efficiency 0.3, tolerance 0.01; eight Metropolis chains with 20% burn-in and Gelman–Rubin tolerance
0.01; four emcee chains of 160 walkers and 12 000 samples [src: App. D, footnotes]. Costs: PolyChord
took about 3 (TATT) or 7 (NLA) times the CPU hours of MultiNest; emcee about 10 times and
Metropolis–Hastings about 1.5 times those of PolyChord, even when started from good proposals
[src: same]. The emcee chains held only about five autocorrelation times each, because the
autocorrelation estimate "was found to be highly sensitive" to the method [src: same]. KiDS-1000 also
reports that MultiNest "struggles to explore" the 18-dimensional galaxy–galaxy lensing posterior and
omits those results (arXiv:2007.01844) [src: Sect. 7].

### Step 6. Calibrate the explicit likelihood itself, as one would a learned posterior
<!-- key: step-6-calibrate-the-explicit-likelihood-itself -->

Vinciguerra, Martinet & Gatti 2026 (arXiv:2607.17942) apply the simulation-based-inference
diagnostics to a Gaussian likelihood with an emulated mean [src: Sect. 6.2, App. C]:

1. Hold out 692 test cosmologies (15% of the simulation set) never used in training or tuning.
2. TARP: for each node the fraction of posterior mass at lower density than the truth; expected
   coverage against credibility over 500 `α` bins, with ±1σ and ±2σ bands from 100 bootstrap
   resamples of the test set.
3. Marginal coverage per parameter, with bootstrap bands.
4. PIT rank histograms with 10^4 posterior samples per node: U-shaped means overconfident, inverted U
   under-confident.
5. Posterior-predictive check of medians against truths across nodes.
6. Realisation-to-realisation test: 20 independent mock observations of the target cosmology; the
   median, mean and MAP biases in units of the posterior width, each shifted by its median over the
   20 [src: Sect. 7.3].

Outcome on MOPED-compressed shear correlation functions: the explicit likelihood with both compressed
bins is under-confident at low-to-intermediate levels in marginal coverage and overconfident in σ_8 by
the rank test; keeping bin 0 only restores calibration, tracing the failure to emulation error and the
non-Gaussianity of bin 1 [src: App. C]. The learned likelihood passes all tests on the same nodes
[src: same].

### Step 7. Calibrate shifts between analysis variants on noisy mocks
<!-- key: step-7-calibrate-shifts-between-analysis -->

Before reading a shift between two variants of the same analysis (scale cut, statistic) as a
systematic, compute how often noise alone produces it. In the DES Y3 + KiDS-1000 analysis a 0.7–0.8σ
rise of the KiDS-1000 S_8 when `θ_min` moves from 0.5′ to 2.0′ is matched by noisy mock KiDS-1000
simulations, in which a shift of at least 0.7σ (0.8σ) occurs 23% (14%) of the time; the most extreme
hydrodynamic simulation tested produces 0.4σ noise-free (arXiv:2305.17173) [src: App. F]. Only a shift
that is rare under noise, and of the sign a modelled contamination predicts, points to a systematic.

## Choices and what they cost
<!-- key: choices-and-what-they-cost -->

| Choice | Option A | Option B | Who | What was found |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Point used to judge recovery | MAP (with PJ-HPD) | 2D marginal against an uncontaminated twin run | KiDS-1000; DES Y3 | KiDS: MAP within 1.7%; DES: MAP too noisy in degenerate models, marginal-against-twin removes the shared projection |
| Data vector | noiseless | ensembles with covariance noise | KiDS-1000, DES Y3 | same conclusions (KiDS); projection stable over 100 realisations (DES) |
| Bias threshold | 0.3 `σ_2D` in a 2D plane | one third of the statistical error per parameter | DES Y3; DESI DR2 Lyα | both set so that the total stays well below the statistical error |
| Goodness-of-fit guard | residual `Δχ² < 1` after refit | χ² distribution of individual mock fits | DES Y3; DESI DR2 Lyα | DESI's excess traced to the model, not the covariance |
| Covariance in the validation | analytic | from 5000 mocks, simple or realistic footprint | KiDS-1000 | "no significant impact on any of the marginal parameter constraints" (runs with `δz` fixed and no shear-bias term) [src: arXiv:2007.01844, Sect. 7, Table 5 IDs 9–12] |
| Amplitude parameter sampled | `ln A_s` | S_8 with a wide flat prior | KiDS-1000 | sampling S_8 widens the 1σ interval by 19%, mostly towards low values [src: Sect. 7] |
| Code check | model-vector `Δχ²` at one point | full cross-fit | DES Y3 | both used; `Δχ² < 0.2` then sets the floor of "insignificant" changes |

Two readings of a threshold. A 0.3σ_2D bias per effect, with all unmodelled effects combined at their
credible maximum (DES Y3), is a bound on the worst case; a σ/3 bias measured on a 400-mock stack
(DESI Lyα) is a measurement of the expected bias, which then enters the systematic budget if it
approaches the threshold. The stacked Lyα bias on `α_∥` is −0.0052 ± 0.0005 for all 400 mocks
[src: arXiv:2503.14741, Table 1], against a threshold of 0.0038 for `α_∥` [src: Fig. 6 caption]; the
text calls it "biased at high significance, with a bias near the threshold criterion", and traces it
to the way redshift errors were added to the mocks [src: Sect. IV.1]. The magnitude read in the
table exceeds the caption's threshold; the paper's wording and these two numbers are not reconciled
in the passages read.

## What goes wrong
<!-- key: what-goes-wrong -->

- **A marginal shifted from the input read as a pipeline bias.** In a noiseless test the marginal can
  move by nearly 1σ through projection alone (arXiv:2105.13548) [src: Sect. IV.1]. Test: the MAP; or
  compare with the twin fit to the uncontaminated vector (Step 3).
- **A MAP too noisy to measure a small bias.** Degenerate models make repeated optimisations disagree
  (arXiv:2105.13548) [src: Sect. V]. Test: restarts; fall back on the twin-run 2D marginal.
- **A validation that hides a code difference.** Model changes smaller than the inter-code residual are
  invisible; without two codes the residual is unknown (arXiv:2105.13548) [src: Sects. IV.1, V].
- **Mocks that lack physics present in the data.** Lyα mocks without non-linear broadening give errors
  10–20% too small (arXiv:2503.14741) [src: Sect. IV.2]; a pipeline validated on them validates the
  wrong error bar. Test: compare the mock error distribution with the data error.
- **A bias from the mocks taken for a bias of the pipeline, or the reverse.** Repeat on raw mocks
  without the pipeline steps (arXiv:2503.14741) [src: Sect. IV.1].
- **A χ² excess blamed on the covariance.** In DESI it came from the model at small scales; Monte Carlo
  from the best-fit model separates the two (arXiv:2503.14741) [src: Sect. IV.2].
- **A sampler that narrows intervals.** MultiNest at the settings used was 12–15% too narrow in S_8
  (arXiv:2305.17173) [src: App. D]. Test: one run with a second sampler on the final likelihood.
- **A shift between analysis variants read as a detection of a systematic.** Noise gives ≥ 0.7σ in 23%
  of KiDS-like mocks (arXiv:2305.17173) [src: App. F]. Test: the noise distribution of the shift for
  nested data.
- **An explicit likelihood assumed calibrated.** Under emulator error and a non-Gaussian compressed bin
  it failed coverage and rank tests that a learned likelihood passed (arXiv:2607.17942) [src: App. C].
  Test: run the same calibration suite on the explicit pipeline.
- **Validation runs simplified for speed.** KiDS-1000 fixed `δz` and dropped the shear-bias term in
  its covariance comparison (arXiv:2007.01844) [src: Sect. 7, footnote]. State every simplification;
  a validation is valid for the configuration it ran.

## Reading list
<!-- key: reading-list -->

- Method and application: Joachimi et al. 2021, arXiv:2007.01844 — noiseless and noisy recovery with
  MAP and PJ-HPD, covariance and prior variations side by side (Sect. 7, Table 5).
- Method and application: Krause et al. 2021, arXiv:2105.13548 — two independent pipelines, the 0.3σ_2D
  and `Δχ² < 1` criteria, importance sampling for small variations, bias measured against an
  uncontaminated twin (Sects. IV.1, V).
- Application: Casas et al. 2025, arXiv:2503.14741 — stacked bias with a σ/3 requirement, raw-mock
  control, per-mock pulls and χ², mock-against-data error check (Sect. IV).
- Application: DES & KiDS 2023, arXiv:2305.17173 — sampler comparison on the final likelihood and the
  noise calibration of a scale-cut shift (Apps. D, F).
- Critique and method: Vinciguerra et al. 2026, arXiv:2607.17942 — coverage, rank and TARP tests applied
  to an explicit Gaussian likelihood (Sects. 5.2, 6.2, 7.3, App. C).
- Related: Lemos et al. 2023, arXiv:2202.08233 — sampler validation on DES Y1 (L2 and the sampler
  dossier).
